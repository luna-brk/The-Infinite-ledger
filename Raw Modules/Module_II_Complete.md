# MODULE II: CURRENCIES & MEDIUMS OF EXCHANGE
## *The Infinite Ledger — Planar Economy System for PF1e Planescape*

---

> *"You want to know the secret of the multiverse? It's not belief, berk. It's not power, and it's sure as the Lady's blades not love. It's liquidity. The being who controls what flows from one plane to another controls everything else."*
> — Shemeshka the Marauder, arcanaloth and currency broker, Sigil

---

## II.0 — MODULE OVERVIEW

This module catalogs every currency, medium of exchange, and store of value in the multiverse — from the copper pip a Cager presses into a pie-seller's palm, to the mythic soul-treasuries controlled by archdevils and demon princes. It is organized from the most universal and stable currencies down to the most exotic, dangerous, and reality-bending. Every entry includes: physical description, denomination structure, who issues it, where it's accepted, how to authenticate it, how to convert it, and what happens when things go wrong.

**This module covers, without omission:**

- The four Hard Currencies that form the backbone of interplanar trade
- Four major Prime Material worlds (Oerth/Greyhawk, Toril/Forgotten Realms, Athas/Dark Sun, Krynn/Dragonlance) with full regional detail and the Underdark as a sub-zone
- Additional Prime worlds for reference (Eberron, Mystara, Birthright, Spelljammer networks)
- The Transitive Planes in full: the Astral, the Ethereal, the Shadow Plane, and the theorized Ordial
- The Feywild and its Courts in detail, with fey-specific currency mechanics
- The Shadowfell and its major markets in detail
- The Domains of Dread as a sealed demiplane-cluster with unique economic laws
- All four Classical Elemental Planes (Air, Earth, Fire, Water) with archomental politics
- All four Para-Elemental Planes (Ice, Magma, Ooze, Smoke)
- All eight Quasi-Elemental Planes (Lightning, Minerals, Radiance, Steam — Positive; Ash, Dust, Salt, Vacuum — Negative)
- The Positive Energy Plane and the Negative Energy Plane
- All sixteen Outer Planes (the Great Ring in full) plus the Outlands and Sigil
- A complete Wealth Spectrum from beggary to cosmic economic dominion — Tiers 1 through 12

**How to use this module:** The Hard Currencies in II.1 are the spine — learn these first. Regional and planar currencies (II.2 through II.5) are reference material for when the party arrives somewhere specific. Metaphysical currencies (II.6) come into play around levels 5–7. Conversion tables (II.7) are your table-side reference. The Wealth Spectrum (II.8) is what "rich" means at every tier, including the ultra-wealthy who field armies, run intelligence networks, and reshape the economic destiny of entire planes.

**PF1e WBL Anchor:** All prices and wealth values in this module are calibrated to PF1e Wealth by Level. The economy contextualizes WBL; it does not violate it. A 10th-level character has approximately 62,000 gp net worth in whatever form that wealth takes. A 20th-level character has approximately 760,000 gp. The ultra-wealthy (Tiers 9–12) are narrative constructs — kings, faction leaders, demon lords — whose wealth exists as a story resource, not as a PC-accessible windfall.

## II.1 — HARD CURRENCIES

Hard currencies are accepted across multiple planes and function as the load-bearing pillars of interplanar commerce. There are four, each backed by different forms of trust. Learn these four currencies before anything else in this module.

### II.1.1 — THE SIGILIAN JINX
*The Reserve Currency of the Multiverse*

#### Physical Description

A jinx is a small, irregularly shaped coin — no two are exactly alike. Each is stamped from an alloy called **dusksteel**, a blue-grey metal smelted exclusively in Sigil from ores gathered across a dozen planes. The obverse bears the symbol of the Merkhant Consortium (an open hand crossed by a key). The reverse bears a shifting, maze-like pattern that is subtly different on every coin — this is **the Lady's Mark**, and it is what makes counterfeiting impossible. The Mark doesn't shimmer or glow; it simply *is wrong* when replicated. Forgeries feel slippery, unsettling. Authentic jinx feel like certainty.

A jinx is slightly smaller than a standard gold piece and weighs roughly the same (50 to the pound, PF1e standard coin weight).

#### Denominations

| Denomination | Name | Value | Color/Marking |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1/100 jinx | Pip | 0.01 gp (= 1 cp) | Dull brown dusksteel, single dot |
| 1/10 jinx | Bit | 0.1 gp (= 1 sp) | Copper-toned dusksteel, single maze line |
| 1 jinx | Jinx (standard) | 1 gp | Blue-grey dusksteel, full maze pattern |
| 5 jinx | Fiver / "Hand" | 5 gp | Silver-washed dusksteel, deeper maze |
| 25 jinx | Quarter-Bar | 25 gp | Gold-washed dusksteel, complex maze |
| 100 jinx | Trade Bar | 100 gp | Rectangular bar, 4 inches, maze on all faces |
| 1,000 jinx | Grand Bar | 1,000 gp | Palm-sized bar, intricate mazes, weighs 1 lb |
| 10,000 jinx | Consortium Plate | 10,000 gp | Plate-sized, 3 lb; requires identity verification at issuance |
| 100,000 jinx | Vault Ingot | 100,000 gp | Brick-sized, 10 lb, serialized, transfer logged in Consortium ledgers |
| 1,000,000 jinx | Foundry Monolith | 1,000,000 gp | Pillar-sized slab, 80 lb; held exclusively in Consortium vaults; transfer is paperwork only |
| 10,000,000 jinx | The Lady's Weight | 10,000,000 gp | Theoretical. Reportedly three have ever been created. Stored in an undisclosed vault under dabus supervision. |

**On higher denominations:** The Grand Bar and above are rarely seen in casual circulation. A Trade Bar is the largest coin most Sigilites handle in a year. Vault Ingots live almost exclusively in Consortium vaults; their transactions are conducted through paperwork rather than physical movement. The Foundry Monolith is the institutional reserve unit of the wealthiest merchant princes, senior faction leaders, and powerful extraplanar lords. The Lady's Weight is more rumor than reality — a denomination that exists in the Consortium's ledger system but whose physical form is perhaps a formality.

#### Issuance & Minting

The Merkhant Consortium holds the exclusive right to mint jinx in Sigil. Minting occurs in the Foundry Quarter, under direct observation by dabus — who apply the Lady's Mark to each coin through a process no mortal has ever successfully observed or replicated. The Consortium pays the Lady's fee for this service, though what that fee consists of is one of the great mysteries of Sigil's economy. The most popular theory: a percentage of all transactions conducted in jinx flows to the Lady as *information*. She doesn't want gold — she wants knowledge of what moves through her city.

New jinx enter circulation through Consortium exchange windows (where foreign currency is converted) and through Consortium lending operations. The total supply of jinx is not public knowledge. The Consortium claims it is carefully managed; the Fated claim it is inflated for Consortium profit; the Fraternity of Order has been trying to audit the Consortium for three centuries and has been politely stonewalled every time.

#### Acceptance

- **Everywhere in Sigil** — universal, no exceptions
- **Throughout the Outlands** — as readily as local coinage, often preferred
- **On most Outer Planes** — with varying degrees of enthusiasm (see plane-specific notes)
- **On the Inner/Elemental Planes** — accepted at major trade posts and crossroads; less so in deep interiors
- **On the Transitive Planes** — accepted at githyanki outposts, astral trading posts, and ethereal waypoints
- **On the Shadowfell** — accepted in Gloomwrought and at established markets; less elsewhere
- **In the Feywild** — accepted cautiously; fey prefer their own currencies or Bargains
- **In the Domains of Dread** — accepted only in cosmopolitan domains; sealed domains refuse or don't recognize them
- **On the Prime Material** — accepted by planar-aware merchants only; to ordinary Primes, a jinx is a strange foreign coin worth approximately 0.5 gp as metal

#### Authentication

| Method | DC | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Casual inspection (familiar handler) | Automatic | Anyone who's handled jinx regularly recognizes them by feel |
| Visual inspection (unfamiliar handler) | Appraise DC 10 | The maze pattern is distinctive |
| Detecting a forgery | Appraise DC 15 | Forgeries feel subtly wrong — a mild unease |
| Detecting a high-quality forgery | Appraise DC 22 | Rare. The maze pattern is almost right but causes faint nausea on close study |
| Magical authentication | Detect Magic | Reveals faint abjuration (the Lady's Mark). Forgeries lack this aura entirely. |

#### Counterfeiting

It has been tried. It always fails. The Lady's Mark is not a spell — it is an expression of the Lady's will, and no known magic replicates it. The most famous attempt was by a cabal of Guvners who spent forty years analyzing the Mark's pattern mathematically. They produced a coin that passed visual inspection and even radiated faint abjuration. The Lady mazed all seven of them on the day they tried to spend it. Their coins are displayed in the Consortium's headquarters as a warning.

**Mechanical ruling:** Counterfeiting jinx is impossible within the scope of this system. If a player insists on attempting it, the DC is ∞. If they find some extraordinary means (a wish, divine intervention), the Lady responds personally. This is a hard boundary.

#### Letters of Credit and Banking Instruments

For transactions above 100 jinx, the Merkhant Consortium issues letters of credit — folded sheets of vellum bearing the Consortium's seal and the Lady's Mark. These function as bank drafts, redeemable at any Consortium exchange window in Sigil or at major offices on the Outlands.

| Service | Fee |
|---|---|
| Issuing a letter of credit (up to 10,000 jinx) | 1% of face value (minimum 1 jinx) |
| Issuing a letter of credit (10,001–100,000 jinx) | 0.5% of face value |
| Issuing a letter of credit (100,001–1,000,000 jinx) | 0.25% of face value |
| Issuing a letter of credit (1,000,001+ jinx) | 0.1% of face value + Consortium board approval |
| Cashing a letter at a Sigil office | Free |
| Cashing a letter on the Outlands | 2% of face value |
| Cashing a letter elsewhere | 5% of face value (if possible at all) |
| Reporting a lost/stolen letter | 10 jinx flat fee + 10% of face value (refunded if recovered) |

Letters of credit are not bearer instruments. They are keyed to a specific individual via magical imprint (a drop of blood, a lock of hair, or a spoken true name — customer's choice). Only the designated recipient can cash them, though formal transfer is possible at a Consortium office for a 3% transfer fee.

#### Vault Account Services

The Consortium offers true banking for substantial wealth:

| Service | Fee | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Opening a Vault Account | 100 jinx flat + identity verification | Minimum balance of 10,000 jinx |
| Maintaining a Vault Account | 0.5% per year of balance | Storage fee |
| Interest on deposits | 1–3% annual (variable) | Only on formal Vault Accounts |
| Wire transfer (between Consortium offices, any plane) | 1% of transferred amount | Processed within 1 business day |
| Secured loan against account | 8–15% annual interest | Based on creditworthiness |
| Unsecured loan | 20–40% annual interest | Only for very established clients |

For the ultra-wealthy (balances exceeding 1,000,000 jinx), the Consortium offers **Private Banking:**

- Dedicated account manager (typically a senior tiefling or aasimar administrator)
- Custom investment portfolios (shares in Consortium ventures, trade syndicate positions, demiplane bonds)
- Personal letter-of-credit courier service (a planar messenger maintains a route to wherever you are)
- Access to the Consortium's most discreet currency-exchange services
- Informal voice in Consortium policy matters
- Priority access to the Great Bazaar's restricted supply tiers
- Sovereign wealth management (for clients managing the finances of entire cities, kingdoms, or planar territories)

### II.1.2 — MINTED STYX-IRON
*The Currency of the Damned*

#### Physical Description

Styx-Iron is exactly what it sounds like: iron dredged from the River Styx and forged in the volcanic workshops of Gehenna. It is black with an oily, iridescent sheen — like a raven's wing dipped in petroleum. It is cold to the touch regardless of ambient temperature and carries a faint psychic residue of the memories the Styx has dissolved. Handling Styx-Iron for extended periods (more than an hour of continuous skin contact) causes mild disorientation and short-term memory fuzziness — nothing mechanically significant for a few hours, but unsettling.

Styx-Iron is minted into flat, rectangular bars of standardized weight. No imagery is stamped — the Lower Planes do not care for symbolism on their currency. Value is determined purely by weight and purity.

#### Denominations

| Denomination | Weight | Value (gp) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Styx Sliver | 1 oz | 5 gp | Small, palm-sized bar. Pocket currency. |
| Styx Bar (standard) | 10 oz | 50 gp | Standard trade unit. About the size of a deck of cards. |
| Styx Ingot | 50 oz | 250 gp | Brick-sized. Serious transactions only. |
| Styx Block | 200 oz | 1,000 gp | Requires two hands to carry. Used between fiendish organizations. |
| Styx Slab | 1,000 oz (62.5 lb) | 5,000 gp | Institutional quantities. Moved on carts. |
| Styx Plinth | 10,000 oz (625 lb) | 50,000 gp | Stored in fortified vaults. Used between Hell's pit lords and major yugoloth companies. |
| Styx Monolith | 100,000 oz (6,250 lb) | 500,000 gp | In practice, held by archdevils and the Oinoloth. Moving one requires a significant operation. |
| Styx Colossal | 1,000,000 oz (62,500 lb) | 5,000,000 gp | Theoretical denomination. Represents the treasury reserve of an entire layer of Hell. Transacted only through paperwork; the physical object is kept in Nessus or Gehenna's deepest vault. |

#### Why It's Valuable

1. **Binding Efficacy.** Chains, shackles, and circles made of Styx-Iron impose a –2 penalty on escape attempts (both mundane and magical) by fiends. Critical security material in the Lower Planes, where betrayal is a way of life.
2. **Memory Erosion.** Weapons forged from Styx-Iron carry a trace of the Styx's memory-devouring properties. A creature struck must make a Will save (DC 12) or lose one round's worth of short-term memory — they forget what happened in the round they were struck.
3. **Planar Stability.** Styx-Iron does not rust, corrode, or degrade on any Lower Plane. It is the most physically durable mundane metal in existence when kept in those environments.

#### Issuance & Sourcing

Styx-Iron is not "issued" by any central authority. It is *harvested* — dragged from the banks and shallows of the River Styx by teams of enslaved petitioners, minor fiends, or desperate mortals. The largest harvesting operations are run by the yugoloths of Gehenna, who control the most productive stretches of river and operate smelting foundries that purify raw Styx-Iron into currency-grade bars. The night hags of the Grey Waste are the second-largest producers and the primary distributors — they carry Styx-Iron across the Lower Planes as part of their soul-trading operations.

#### Acceptance

- **Lower Planes** — Universal. The default currency across Baator, Gehenna, the Grey Waste, Carceri, the Abyss, and Pandemonium.
- **Sigil** — Accepted by the Merkhant Consortium at standard exchange rates. Many Sigil merchants accept it directly with a 5–10% premium for the inconvenience.
- **Outlands** — Accepted at gate-towns near Lower Plane portals (Ribcage, Torch, Hopeless, Plague-Mort, Curst, Automata's black market). Not accepted in Upper Plane-aligned gate-towns.
- **Domains of Dread** — Accepted in many domains, particularly those with fiendish connections.
- **Upper Planes** — Not accepted. Styx-Iron radiates faint evil; celestial merchants refuse to touch it on principle.
- **Prime Material** — Functionally unknown. If identified (Knowledge [planes] DC 18), it can be sold to planar-aware buyers at base gp value.
- **Shadowfell** — Accepted in darker markets; Gloomwrought takes it at a 10% discount.

### II.1.3 — CELESTIAN TRADE BARS
*The Currency of the Righteous*

#### Physical Description

Celestian Trade Bars are slim rectangles of sanctified silver, approximately five inches long, one inch wide, and a quarter-inch thick. Each bears the seal of the Celestian Trade Concordance — a handshake beneath a radiant sun — impressed into both faces. The silver has a warm, faintly luminous quality; it doesn't glow visibly in daylight, but in dim light or darkness, a Celestian Trade Bar emits light equivalent to a candle in a 5-foot radius.

The bars are warm to the touch for good-aligned creatures, neutral-temperature for neutral creatures, and uncomfortably cold for evil-aligned creatures. This property is the bar's primary authentication mechanism.

#### Denominations

| Denomination | Value (gp) | Physical Form | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Celestian Sliver | 10 gp | Thin strip, 2 inches long | Everyday transactions on Upper Planes |
| Celestian Trade Bar (standard) | 100 gp | Full bar, 5 inches | Standard unit |
| Celestian Grand Bar | 500 gp | Larger bar, 8 inches, thicker | Major transactions |
| Celestian Covenant Seal | 1,000 gp | Circular medallion, palm-sized | Issued only for specific large contracts |
| Celestian Sanctum Plate | 10,000 gp | Silver disc, 1 foot diameter | Used for major temple endowments and institutional transfers |
| Celestian Throne Bar | 100,000 gp | Large plate-like ingot, 5 lb | Treaty settlements between realms; planar-scale transfers |
| Celestian Crown Ingot | 1,000,000 gp | Heavy bar, 25 lb, consecrated | Reserved for the most senior Upper Plane institutional transactions; each is individually blessed by an archon of the highest rank |

#### Issuance

Celestian Trade Bars are issued by the **Celestian Trade Concordance**, a cooperative body representing major celestial commercial interests across the Upper Planes. The Concordance includes representatives from Mount Celestia (archon bankers), Bytopia (gnomish crafters and traders), Elysium (agricultural exporters), and the Beastlands (natural resource managers). Arborea and Ysgard participate intermittently.

#### Acceptance

- **Upper Planes** — Universal. The standard currency of the Upper Planes.
- **Sigil** — Accepted by the Merkhant Consortium at standard rates.
- **Outlands** — Accepted near Upper Plane gate-towns; less so near Lower Plane gates.
- **Prime Material** — Accepted by temple-affiliated merchants, particularly in communities with strong celestial ties.
- **Lower Planes** — Refused by evil entities (alignment revulsion). Neutral evil merchants may accept at 20% discount for the inconvenience of handling it.

#### Authentication

The alignment-temperature response is the key authentication mechanism:

| Creature Alignment | Bar feels... | DC to fake this property |
|---|---|---|
| Good | Warm, comforting | Cannot be faked; property is magical |
| Neutral | Room temperature | — |
| Evil | Cold, uncomfortable | — |

Additional authentication: **Detect Good** reveals a strong aura. Forgeries made of ordinary silver lack the aura entirely and feel neutral regardless of handler alignment.

### II.1.4 — QUINTESSENCE
*The Currency of the Astral*

#### Physical Description

Quintessence is the solidified residue of thought and time that drifts through the Astral Plane — wisps of silver-grey matter that have condensed from the psychic medium of the plane. When harvested and refined, it becomes a thick, cloudy liquid that moves like mercury but glows faintly with pearlescent light. It is stored in sealed vials of crystal or specially treated glass. Contact with most surfaces causes it to slowly dissolve, which is why storage quality matters enormously.

#### Denominations and Value

| Form | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dram (smallest vial) | 10 gp | The base unit. About 1/8th teaspoon. |
| Standard Vial | 40 gp | Standard trade unit; 4 drams |
| Flask | 200 gp | 20 drams; a significant store of value |
| Phial (sealed crystal) | 500 gp | Premium storage; decay rate significantly reduced |
| Distilled Quintessence (flask) | 1,000 gp | Refined for purity; slower decay; preferred by high-end buyers |
| Philosopher's Vial (great crystal) | 10,000 gp | Extraordinary purity; used as component for the highest magical workings |
| Astral Sovereign (sealed monument) | 100,000 gp | A vessel the size of an urn containing near-perfectly preserved quintessence of extraordinary purity. One of the rarest tradeable commodities in existence; held only by githyanki Queen's treasury and a few astral merchant princes. |

#### The Decay Problem

Quintessence is uniquely perishable: it evaporates at a rate determined by storage quality and plane:

| Condition | Daily Decay Rate |
|---|---|
| On the Astral Plane, in standard vessel | 0% (stable) |
| On the Astral Plane, in open air | 5% per hour |
| On the Prime Material, in good vessel | 5% per day |
| On the Prime Material, in poor vessel | 15% per day |
| On any Elemental Plane | 10% per day |
| On any Outer Plane | 3% per day |
| In a Githyanki Stability Matrix (specialized container) | 0% regardless of plane |

#### Properties

Quintessence has genuine magical utility beyond its currency value:
- **Time Suspension:** A creature or object immersed in quintessence ceases to age, hunger, thirst, bleed, or change until removed. This makes it invaluable for preserving living specimens, sustaining wounded companions, or suspending magical processes indefinitely.
- **Astral Travel Aid:** Quintessence amplifies astral projection, reducing the casting time of *astral projection* by half when consumed as a material component.
- **Magical Catalyst:** Used as a spell component for high-level spells involving time, prophecy, memory, or the mind.

#### Acceptance

- **Astral Plane** — Universal among those who know what it is.
- **Sigil** — Accepted by the Consortium and specialty magical merchants.
- **Githyanki markets** — Premium currency; they produce and store the most.
- **Outer Planes** — Accepted by planar-aware traders; most petitioners don't know what it is.
- **Prime Material** — Known only to specialists. If identified, valuable; if not, a mysterious glowing liquid.

### II.1.5 — THE ORDIAL RESONANCE FRAGMENT
*Currency of the Dead Between Planes (Rare; Theoretical)*

The Ordial Plane — the rumored transitive plane connecting the Outer Planes to each other and to the energy planes — is theorized but not definitively mapped. Those who claim to have traversed it describe fragments of crystallized reality that form at the intersections of planar boundaries: **Ordial Resonance Fragments**, sometimes called **Border-Stones** by those who deal in them.

These fragments are not confirmed to be a stable currency — their existence and reliability vary by who you ask. What is known:

| Item | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ordial Resonance Fragment (minor) | 500–2,000 gp | A small crystalline shard; used as a planar-travel component |
| Ordial Resonance Fragment (major) | 5,000–20,000 gp | Larger, more coherent; used in planar cartography and transit magic |
| Ordial Meridian (the largest recorded) | 100,000+ gp | A fragment the size of a fist; allegedly shows the relative positions of nearby planes when held |

**GM note:** Whether the Ordial truly exists as a navigable plane is a campaign-level mystery. Ordial Fragments as currency represent the bleeding edge of extraplanar commerce — available only to characters deeply embedded in planar politics and exploration.

## II.2 — PRIME MATERIAL CURRENCIES

The Prime Material Plane contains thousands of worlds, each with its own economic history. This section covers four major worlds in depth, followed by secondary world references and the Underdark as a sub-zone.

**General rule for Prime world currencies:** In their home nations, they function at face value. On other Prime worlds, they are typically worth their metal value (the gp value of the coin's raw metal content). To planar traders in Sigil or the Outlands, many Prime coins are simply "gold" or "silver" by weight.

---

### II.2.1 — OERTH (Greyhawk)
*Where the Adventure Begins*

Oerth is the campaign's starting world — a broadly standard late-medieval fantasy economy. Its currencies are recognizable to players accustomed to PF1e and serve as the baseline from which all other economic systems deviate.

#### Standard Oerth Currency (PF1e Standard)

| Coin | Material | Value | Weight (50/lb) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper Piece (cp) | Copper | 0.01 gp | 50/lb |
| Silver Piece (sp) | Silver | 0.1 gp | 50/lb |
| Gold Piece (gp) | Gold | 1 gp | 50/lb |
| Platinum Piece (pp) | Platinum | 10 gp | 50/lb |

This is the PF1e standard, and it applies to most of Oerth. Where deviation exists, it is noted below.

#### Regional Oerth Currencies

| Nation/Region | Coin Name | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Free City of Greyhawk | Standard gp/sp/cp | 1 gp standard | Well-minted; accepted throughout the Flanaess |
| Furyondy | Furyondian Crown | 1 gp | Gold, bears the king's face; respected trade currency |
| Furyondy | Silver Crown | 0.1 gp | |
| Keoland | Keoish Mark | 1 gp | Slightly larger; heavier than standard |
| Veluna | Velunan Solar | 1 gp | Religious imagery; valued by Rao's faithful |
| Iuz (Empire of) | Iuz Iron | 0.8 gp | Debased coinage; 80% value outside Iuz territory |
| Scarlet Brotherhood | Hidden Coinage | 1 gp (face) | Secretly marked; Brotherhood agents recognize each other through specific scratches on these coins |
| Ull (steppe) | Ull Ingots | varies by weight | No coins; wealth stored as metal ingots |
| Sea Princes | Sea Marks | 1 gp | Nautical imagery; accepted in coastal ports |
| Ulek States | Ulek Standard | 1 gp | Dwarf-assured minting; extremely reliable |
| Verbobonc | Verbobonc Half-Crown | 0.5 gp | Common fractional denomination in this trade city |
| Dyvers | Dyvers Florin | 1 gp | Commercial city; actively maintained exchange infrastructure |
| Nyrond | Nyrond Stag | 1 gp | Stag imagery; currently inflated due to ongoing wars |
| Great Kingdom (Aerdi) | Imperial Orb | 1 gp | Declining in trust due to political instability |

#### Oerth Regional Banking

- **City of Greyhawk:** Major merchant houses (House Oeridian, the Merchants' and Traders' Union) offer letters of credit valid within the Flanaess. Temple banking through Zilchus's churches provides formal deposit and interest services.
- **Furyondy:** Royal Treasury issues letters of credit for large transactions.
- **Veluna:** Temple banking through the See of Veluna; spiritually backed, reliable.
- **Dwarven lands (Ulek Principality, Iron Hills):** The most reliable vault banking on Oerth; dwarven banks have not failed in recorded history. Interest rates are modest (1–2% annually) but the institutions are as solid as the mountains they're carved into.

#### Oerth Trade Goods as Currency

In frontier regions, trade goods substitute for coinage:
- Furs (beaver, sable): 1–10 gp each depending on quality
- Spices (pepper, saffron, cinnamon): 5–50 gp per lb
- Quality iron: 1 gp per lb (lower in dwarven regions)
- Enchanted items: At standard PF1e prices (consult Module III for regional modifiers)

#### Planar Interface

Most Oerthians have no knowledge of planar commerce. To a merchant in Greyhawk, a Sigilian jinx is a strange foreign coin and is assessed at roughly 0.5 gp (its estimated metal value). Planar-aware merchants (typically those with Knowledge [planes] ranks or guild affiliations with planar contacts) recognize jinx at full value.

The **Transcendent Order's** small presence on Oerth (primarily a monastery outside Greyhawk) provides the closest thing Oerth has to a currency exchange for interplanar trade — though they offer it as a public service, not a commercial operation.

### II.2.2 — TORIL (Forgotten Realms)
*The World of Ten Thousand Merchants*

Toril is the most commercially developed Prime world in this system. Its merchant guilds, banking houses (the temples of Waukeen, Gond, and Lliira in particular), and multi-city letter-of-credit systems represent the most sophisticated financial infrastructure on any Prime world. Toril's major cities rival mid-level Sigil commerce.

#### The Faerûnian Standard

| Coin | Material | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper Piece (cp) | Copper | 0.01 gp | Standard |
| Silver Piece (sp) | Silver | 0.1 gp | Standard |
| Electrum Piece (ep) | Electrum | 0.5 gp | Common in Faerûn; rare elsewhere |
| Gold Piece (gp) | Gold | 1 gp | Standard |
| Platinum Piece (pp) | Platinum | 10 gp | Standard |

The electrum piece is Faerûn's unique denomination — a 50/50 gold-silver alloy that bridges the gap between silver and gold. Not all merchants outside Faerûn recognize it; treat as 0.5 gp in value everywhere, but some traders will haggle.

| Amn | Amnian Camel Mark | 1 gp | Camel on obverse; trade-focused |

#### Toril Banking Infrastructure

Toril has the most sophisticated Prime Material banking in the multiverse:

**Temples of Waukeen:** The gold-standard of Faerûnian banking. Before Waukeen's disappearance (and after her return), the Church of Waukeen operated branches in every major city, offering:
- Letter-of-credit services valid across Faerûn
- Deposit accounts with 1–2% annual interest
- Currency exchange (all standard coins, 2–3% fee)
- Trade financing (secured loans at 8–12%)
- Merchant-bonding (a temple-guaranteed guarantee of a merchant's creditworthiness)

**Major Merchant Houses** (Amn, Sembia): Operate proprietary letter-of-credit networks that are accepted at affiliated houses across the continent. More limited geographically than temple banking but sometimes faster and more discreet.

**Dwarf Clan Banking** (the Iron Throne, Thunder Blessing): The most secure vault storage in the Realms. Dwarven institutions charge 1% annual storage but guarantee the contents against any non-divine loss.

**House of the Wise Coin** (Waterdeep): The largest single financial institution in the Realms, run by a consortium of Waterdhavian nobles. Provides letters of credit valid in any city with a Waterdhavian embassy or allied merchant guild — which is most of the civilized world.

#### Planar Interface

Toril has more planar commerce than any other Prime world. The City of Portals (Sigil) does a meaningful fraction of its business with Toril-origin merchants. The Harpers, the Zhentarim, and the Red Wizards all maintain planar contacts. Several Toril-based merchants operate permanent stalls in Sigil's Great Bazaar.

Torilians recognize jinx at their full value almost universally in major cities. Conversely, well-minted Waterdhavian gold pieces circulate in Sigil at a 5% discount (recognizable as quality metal, but requiring conversion from an unfamiliar mint).

### II.2.3 — ATHAS (Dark Sun)
*The Dying World*

Athas is unlike any other Prime world in this system. It is a world of ecological devastation, where centuries of reckless arcane magic (defiling) have stripped the land of most life, turned forests to desert, and left the water scarce and the metal scarcer. Its economy is built around fundamental scarcity rather than abundance, which inverts nearly every standard economic assumption.

**The Core Economic Inversion:** On Athas, metal is so rare that ceramic has replaced coin as the primary currency. A genuine iron sword is a major piece of wealth. Mithral and adamantine are essentially mythical — encountered only in the most extraordinary circumstances.

#### Athasian Currency: The Ceramic Standard

| Denomination | Material | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ceramic Bit (smallest) | Fired clay | 0.005 gp | The smallest practical unit |
| Ceramic Piece | Fired clay | 0.01 gp | The primary small currency (= 1 cp equivalent) |
| Ceramic Piece (5-unit) | Fired clay, marked | 0.05 gp | |
| Ceramic Piece (10-unit) | Fired clay, marked | 0.1 gp | Common daily commerce |
| Ceramic Trade Piece | Heavy fired clay | 0.5 gp | For significant purchases |
| Ceramic Crown | Fine glazed clay | 1 gp | Premium denomination; rare outside sorcerer-kings' cities |
| Metal Coin (iron or bronze) | Iron/Bronze | 10 gp per coin | Treated as precious; face value irrelevant — it's metal |
| Metal Ingot (1 lb) | Iron | 100 gp | Near-priceless; traded very carefully |
| True Metal (silver, 1 oz) | Silver | 500 gp | Rarely seen outside sorcerer-kings' treasuries |
| True Metal (gold, 1 oz) | Gold | 1,000 gp | The sorcerer-kings' reserve currency; ordinary Athasians have never touched gold |
| Mythic Metal (mithral, 1 oz) | Mithral | 10,000+ gp | Effectively artifact-tier material on Athas |

#### City-State Ceramic Coinage

Each of Athas's sorcerer-king cities issues its own ceramic coinage. While all ceramic pieces have the same intrinsic value (negligible — they're clay), the issuing city's stamp carries political trust:

| City-State | Ceramic Marking | Issuer |
|---|---|---|
| Tyr | Ceramic marked with broken chains | King's Council (post-liberation) |
| Balic | Ceramic marked with a wave | Andropinis (dictator) |

| Draj | Ceramic marked with bleeding sun | Tectuktitlay |

| Gulg | Ceramic marked with a flower wreath | Lalali-Puy |
| Raam | Ceramic in chaos (multiple marks) | Currently contested — political chaos |

**Cross-city friction:** Ceramic pieces from one city-state are accepted in another at 90% value (10% friction). This is intentional — it favors local commerce.

#### Athas-Specific Wealth Storage

The ultra-wealthy on Athas (templars, sorcerer-king officials, high merchant houses) store wealth in:

- **Metal ingots** — the ultimate store of value; one pound of iron is worth a small fortune
- **Slave holdings** — slavery is normalized on Athas; a skilled slave may represent 500–5,000 gp in value
- **Water rights** — controlling a well or oasis is equivalent to owning gold mines
- **Silk Wyrm weave** — a rare textile worth enormous sums
- **Preserved food stores** — long-term survival assets in a dying world
- **Psionic knowledge** — crystallized techniques, not strictly wealth, but valuable

#### Planar Interface

For planar travelers, Athas is a treasure trove and a nightmare simultaneously. A mithral dagger that would fetch 300 gp in Sigil can sell for 3,000–5,000 Athasian gp equivalent in Balic or Tyr. Adamantine is nearly worship-worthy. Conversely, bringing Athasian ceramic "coins" to Sigil yields only their clay value — essentially nothing.

**The Water Trade:** Planar travelers who can reliably bring clean water to Athas (via *create water* services, portable wells, or water-aspected magic) can command extraordinary prices. A half-hour of *create water* castings, producing 50 gallons, can feed an entire Athasian village for a week and sell for the equivalent of 200–500 gp in metal and trade goods.

**Elemental Priests:** Athas's unique relationship to the elemental planes means that elemental priests are plentiful and offer elemental services at lower rates than elsewhere. Water-elemental summoning services are particularly valuable.

### II.2.4 — KRYNN (Dragonlance)
*The World of Steel*

Krynn's economic distinctiveness: steel is more valuable than gold. This inverts standard assumptions and creates unique trade dynamics. During and after the Cataclysm, metal became scarce, and steel — with its practical utility — surpassed gold as the standard of value.

#### The Krynnish Steel Standard

| Currency | Material | Value in standard PF1e gp | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper Piece | Copper | 0.01 gp | Same as PF1e standard |
| Silver Piece | Silver | 0.1 gp | Same as PF1e standard |
| Gold Piece (Krynnish) | Gold | 0.5 gp | Gold is devalued on Krynn |
| Steel Piece | Steel | 1 gp (= standard gp) | The primary currency |
| Platinum Piece | Platinum | 5 gp | Same multiplier over steel as elsewhere over gold |

On Krynn, a steel piece is what a gold piece would be on Oerth. A gold piece on Krynn is worth half a "gp" in the PF1e sense.

**For planar travelers:** Selling steel on Krynn at Krynnish rates and buying gold (which is less valued locally) can be arbitraged back to worlds where gold is primary. The margin is roughly 50% gross — but transport costs, suspicion of foreign meddlers, and the attention drawn by bulk metal movements limit the practice significantly.

#### Kender Economics (Special Note)

Kender are infamous for "handling" (liberating) items that catch their interest. Kender communities have enormous variety of small objects that individual kender have acquired through "handling." Kender settlements often host impromptu markets where genuinely rare items appear for trivial prices because the kender doesn't recognize their value.

**PC opportunity:** Visit a kender market. Find extraordinary things. Pay laughable prices. The ethical question of whether those items were stolen from someone else is left to the PC's conscience. Consistent GM finding: kender markets contain 1d6 items of unusual provenance per visit; roll on a small random table for what's available.

#### Krynnish Banking

Krynnish institutional banking is modest compared to Toril:

- **Palanthas merchant houses:** Letter-of-credit services within Ansalon; limited cross-city acceptance
- **Solamnic Knights' treasuries:** Secure storage with rigorous ethical vetting (alignment LG preferred)
- **Dwarven banks of Thorbardin:** The most reliable banking on Krynn; dwarves don't forget debts and don't lose deposits
- **Elven trust-holdings in Silvanesti:** Formal but accessible only to those the elves trust (Diplomacy DC 18+ and established relationship)

#### Dragon Hoards as Wealth Reference

On Krynn, dragon hoards are the most famous wealth concentrations. A typical adult red dragon hoard on Krynn runs 25,000–75,000 gp (in steel pieces, gems, and magic items). An ancient dragon's hoard may reach 200,000–500,000 gp. These are reference points for what "extremely wealthy" looks like in a Krynnish context.

## II.3 — THE TRANSITIVE PLANES

The Transitive Planes are the spaces between other planes — connective tissue of the cosmology. They are sparsely populated and economically marginal in themselves, but they are the highways along which interplanar commerce moves. Understanding their economies means understanding the costs of moving anything across the multiverse.

---

### II.3.1 — THE ASTRAL PLANE
*Silver Void Between the Outer Planes*

The Astral is an infinite silver sea of solidified thought, populated by drifting god-corpses, githyanki fortresses, planar travelers, and the occasional merchant who has set up a waystation. Currency-wise, the Astral is simple: hard currencies only, no local scrip, and the specialty currency is Quintessence (see II.1.4).

**Economic Character:**
- Default currency: Hard currencies (primarily jinx and Quintessence)
- No native currency beyond Quintessence
- Barter supplements hard currency at smaller posts
- Githyanki communities operate their own internal currency (Silver Sword Bonds, below)

**Githyanki Currency:**

| Currency | Value | Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Silver Sword Bond | 500 gp | A paper bond issued by a githyanki war-band captain, redeemable at the nearest githyanki fortress | The functional internal currency of githyanki military operations |
| Warrior's Mark | 50 gp | A marked piece of silver representing one victory credit within githyanki ranking | Social as much as economic; accumulating Warrior's Marks improves your standing |
| Queen's Seal | 10,000 gp | Issued by Vlaakith's court for extraordinary services | Near-mythic in circulation; only a few hundred exist |
| Astral Cartography Note | 100–5,000 gp | A documented route through the Astral, including hazards and waypoints | Knowledge currency; a reliable route note can be resold repeatedly |

**Major Astral Markets:**
- **Tu'narath (Githyanki Capital):** The largest market on the Astral; deals in weapons, military supplies, and loot from raided planes. Hostile to non-githyanki without introduction.
- **Drifting Markets (temporary):** Merchants who recognized that the Astral's traffic is predictable enough to set up mobile waypoints. A drifting market moves slowly through the Astral on a circuit; experienced travelers know roughly where it will be.
- **God-Isle Markets:** Where a dead deity's corpse has attracted a settlement, ad hoc markets develop. Goods sold here often include divine artifacts and strange materials from the god's portfolio.

**Quintessence Stability Matrix:** Githyanki-made containers (DC 22 Craft [alchemy] to construct; 1,000 gp base cost) hold Quintessence at 0% decay rate regardless of plane. A profitable trade item in itself — the containers sell for 1,500–2,500 gp in Sigil.

---

### II.3.2 — THE ETHEREAL PLANE
*The Mist Between Material and Inner*

The Ethereal is the connective space between the Prime Material worlds and the Inner Planes. It is sparsely populated — mostly phase spiders, ether-dwelling creatures, and the occasional wizard conducting ethereal research. Commerce is minimal but not absent.

**Economic Character:**
- No native currency whatsoever
- Barter dominates at the few waystations
- Accepted currencies: Hard currencies only
- Special trade goods: Drift-stone, ghost-silk (from ethereal-adjacent hauntings), materials harvested from ethereal creatures (phase spider silk fetches 50 gp/yard)

**Phase Spider Silk:** The Ethereal's primary trade good. Phase spiders weave silk as they move between the Material and Ethereal; this silk retains partial phase properties:
- Phase Spider Silk (1 yard): 50 gp
- Phase Spider Silk Bolt (10 yards): 450 gp (bulk discount)
- Woven Phase Cloth (garment): 500–2,000 gp depending on quality; grants +2 circumstance bonus on saves vs. ethereal effects

**Ethereal Waystations:** Small, ad hoc stopping points maintained by various factions. Notable:
- **The Gossamer Helm:** A former sailing ship that became ethereal during a storm and never returned. Now a waystation run by its ghost crew; accepts hard currency and phase spider silk as payment.
- **The Blind Hermit's Post:** Run by an aasimar who chose to live on the Ethereal as a form of meditation. He sells basic supplies at cost and asks only for conversation.

### II.3.3 — THE SHADOW PLANE (Plane of Shadow)
*Dark Mirror of the Material*

The Shadow Plane is a full transitive plane, not merely a pocket — a dark reflection of the Material Plane in which all colors are muted, all light is diminished, and the emotional resonance of loss permeates everything. It is sometimes treated as synonymous with the Shadowfell in this system (see II.3.4 for the Shadowfell specifically); in 1e/Planescape cosmology, the Plane of Shadow is the transitive plane while the Shadowfell is its larger cultural and ecological expression.

**Economic Character:**
- Sparsely populated; dark, grim, quiet
- Shadow-aspected goods are native here; bright goods are luxury imports
- Traffic is largely merchants moving quietly between planes using the Shadow as a corridor

**Primary Currencies:**

| Currency | Value | Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nightshilling | 1 NS = 1 gp | Silver coin that absorbs light; appears dim regardless of illumination | The currency of the Shadowfell (below); circulates on the Shadow Plane as well |
| Shadow-Silk Bolt | 50 gp/yard | Dark, shimmering fabric that resists light | The Shadow Plane's primary export commodity |
| Memory of Sunlight | 10–500 gp | A crystallized memory of daylight (see Memory Pearls, II.6.3) | Prized in the Shadowfell because genuine sunlight is inaccessible |
| Darkness Vial | 5–30 gp | Vial of captured magical darkness; non-dissipating | Useful for stealth operations; component for darkness spells |

### II.3.4 — THE SHADOWFELL
*The Plane of Shadow Realized*

The Shadowfell — in this system used as the cultural and populated extension of the Shadow Plane — is a full plane with distinct economy: darker, grimmer, more stable than pure ether. Its inhabitants include fetchlings (shadow-adapted humanoids), shadar-kai (shadow-touched elves in some configurations), undead of various kinds, and occasional refugees from the Material Plane who find the Shadowfell's bleakness preferable to what they fled.

**Economic Character:**
- Slower than the Prime (things degrade but not quickly)
- Memory-focused (the past has weight; old things hold value)
- Fear-adjacent (drops of dread and shadow-magic components have markets)
- Fetchling-dominated merchant class
- Undead-inclusive economy (intelligent undead are full economic participants)

#### Shadowfell Currencies

| Shadow-Silver (sp equivalent) | 0.1 gp | Silver coins with blackened finish; darker than normal | Regional small denomination |

| Memory of Sunlight | 10–500 gp | Crystallized memory of daylight (see II.6.3) | Prized in the Shadowfell because genuine sunlight is inaccessible |
| Drop of Dread | 5–50 gp | Distilled fear from a sentient creature; used in shadow-magic | Morally complicated; harvested from living or dead creatures |
| Raven Queen's Seal | 100 gp | A wax seal impressed with the Raven Queen's symbol; used as both currency and authorization token | Rare but valuable; issued in Letherna |
| Shroud-Gold | 5 gp/coin | Gold coins that have been through a death ceremony; carry a minor death aura | Premium within the Shadowfell; uncomfortable for living creatures to handle |

#### Shadowfell Economic Mechanics

**Memory-Value:** On the Shadowfell, items associated with significant memories command premium prices — a sword that belonged to a famous warrior is worth more here than its mundane equivalent, and that premium is proportional to how significant the memory is. Appraise checks to assess memory-value require Knowledge (history) DC 15–25.

**Undead Commerce:** Intelligent undead are full economic participants. Vampires are among the wealthiest beings in the Shadowfell — they accumulate wealth over centuries of predation and commerce. Liches typically hold their wealth in magical components.

**Grief as Currency:** In some Shadowfell markets, particularly those near the Raven Queen's domain, demonstrated grief (for a specific, named dead being) functions as a form of payment for information or services related to the deceased. This is not metaphorical — the local magic can tell genuine grief from performance.

### II.3.5 — THE DOMAINS OF DREAD
*The Demiplane of Terror*

The Domains of Dread are not a single plane but a cluster of demiplanes — pocket realities controlled by the **Dark Powers**, ancient entities of unclear nature and unclear motivation. Each domain is ruled by a **Darklord**, a being of significant power who has been drawn into the Demiplane and is simultaneously its ruler and its prisoner. The Darklords are trapped; they cannot leave. Many don't know it yet.

**Economic Character: The Sealed Economy**

The Domains of Dread are economically isolated by design. The Dark Powers seal their creation:

- **Exit is impossible** for most beings (including PCs who are not on specific quest lines, at the GM's discretion)
- **External currency** either doesn't work or is assessed only at metal value
- **Local economies** function only within their domain
- **Cross-domain trade** exists but is limited and hazardous

This isolation is not metaphorical. A PC who arrives in the Domains of Dread carrying 10,000 jinx will find that jinx are foreign objects worth their metal (roughly 0.5 gp each) in most domains — or, in the more paranoid domains, evidence of planar origin that attracts dangerous attention.

#### The Dark Currencies

| Currency | Value | Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dread-Mark | 1 gp equivalent | The most widespread cross-domain currency; dark grey coin of unknown metal, issued by... someone | Nobody knows who issues Dread-Marks. They appear. They work. That's enough for most residents. |
| Domain-Specific Coin | 1 gp equivalent | Each domain mints or uses its own currency | See domain table below |
| Fear-Token | 5–50 gp | A crystallized fragment of intense fear, harvested from a being experiencing genuine terror | Traded in darker corners of the Domains; the Dark Powers find them valuable |
| Dread-Debt | variable | An obligation placed by a Darklord or significant domain power | Never take one lightly; Darklords collect |

#### Domain-Specific Currencies

| Domain | Darklord | Primary Currency | Economic Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barovia | Strahd von Zarovich | Barovian Crest (1 gp equiv., silver) | Feudal; Strahd's treasury is enormous but inaccessible; peasant economy is barter-adjacent |
| Falkovnia | Vlad Drakov | Falcon Standard (1 gp equiv., copper-heavy) | Military-controlled; commerce is heavily taxed |
| Lamordia | Adam (the created being) | Lamordia Mechanist Token (1 gp equiv., brass) | Industrial-gothic economy; clockwork goods trade |
| Mordent | Lord Wilfred Godefroy | Mordentshire Standard (1 gp equiv.) | Upper-class Victorian-analogue; property and reputation matter more than coin |
| Ravenloft Proper | (Strahd's extended domain) | Barovian currency with local variations | — |
| Valachan | Chakuna (current) | Jungle Barter + valachan hunter-marks | Trophy-based economy among hunting-cult |
| Hazlan | Hazlik (Red Wizard refugee) | Hazlani Arcane Token | Magic-economy; spells as primary trade good |
| Darkon | Azalin Rex | Darkon Crown (1 gp equiv.) | The most "normal" domain economy; Azalin maintains stable governance when not preoccupied with escape |
| Sithicus | Lord Soth | Dark Knight Standard | Near-collapsed economy; Soth doesn't care about commerce |
| Bluetspur | The Elder Brain | Psionic Obligation | No material currency; entirely psionic obligation system |

#### Entering the Domains with Wealth

PCs who enter the Domains of Dread with significant outside wealth face immediate practical problems:

1. **Recognition:** Jinx and Trade Bars mark you as an outsider. In most domains, this is dangerous — outsiders are feared, persecuted, or hunted.
2. **Conversion loss:** Converting hard currency to local currency in the Domains typically costs 20–30%.
3. **Exit restriction:** Wealth accumulated in the Domains cannot easily leave (the Dark Powers' seal applies to material goods as well as persons, at GM discretion).
4. **Darklord's cut:** The Darklords are aware of significant wealth concentrations in their domains. This awareness is not immediate, but it exists.

**GM Guidance:** The Domains of Dread should feel economically claustrophobic. Players used to the free commerce of Sigil should experience the sealed economy as a form of the Domains' horror — not just monsters, but the existential dread of a world that won't let you leave, including your money.

### II.3.6 — THE FEYWILD (Plane of Faerie)
*The Bright Mirror of the Material*

The Feywild is the Material Plane's luminous reflection — a realm where every forest is ancient, every meadow is magic, every river has a name and opinions, and the rules of time and reality are suggestions rather than laws. Its economy is correspondingly alien: not corrupt, not broken, just operating on fundamentally different assumptions than mortal economics.

**The Core Feywild Economic Principle:** In the Feywild, *what something means* matters as much as what it *is*. A sword worth 300 gp in Sigil might be worth 3,000 gp in the Feywild if it was forged on a specific festival night — or 30 gp if it's iron (which the fey find uncomfortable) and was made by someone with poor intent. Value is contextual, narrative, and frankly arbitrary by mortal standards.

#### The Fey Bargain System

The Feywild's most distinctive currency is not a coin — it is a **Fey Bargain**: a formal agreement between a mortal and a fey being. Bargains operate on their own rules:

**Rules of Fey Bargains:**
1. A bargain made with a fey is metaphysically binding. The fey cannot break it; neither can the mortal without consequence.
2. The letter of the bargain is binding, not the spirit. Fey are known for exploiting loopholes — PCs should craft bargains carefully.
3. Bargains can be transferred, sold, or inherited.
4. Breaking a bargain does not destroy it — it transforms it into a **Curse Obligation**, which the fey will collect eventually.
5. The value of a Fey Bargain scales with what is traded: a promise of "one service" from a powerful fey is worth a fortune; a promise of "one service" from a pixie is worth a single favor-level 2.

**Valuing Fey Bargains as Currency:**

The Feywild is politically divided between the Summer Court (Seelie) and Winter Court (Unseelie), and this division has economic consequences:

#### Fey Market Rules

The floating fey markets that drift between courts operate under specific rules:

1. **Iron is banned.** Iron weapons and tools cannot be brought to most fey markets. Attempting to do so will trigger an immediate — and possibly violent — response.
2. **Time is different.** A PC who spends "an afternoon" in a fey market may emerge to find a week, a month, or a year has passed. All purchases made should be noted with this risk in mind.
3. **Flattery has economic value.** Sincere compliments about a fey merchant's goods can reduce prices by 5–15%. (Insincere flattery achieves the opposite effect — fey can always tell.)
4. **Names have power.** Giving your true name to a fey merchant grants them leverage. Don't do it. Use a title or a nickname.
5. **Prices are personal.** The same item may cost different amounts for different buyers depending on what the fey merchant wants from each customer.

#### Feywild Economic Integration with Other Planes

- **Sigil:** Feywild goods (moonstone jewelry, fey-woven textiles, dreaming gems) command a premium of 20–50% in Sigil's markets due to their exotic nature and the difficulty of transport.
- **Material Plane:** Feywild goods brought to the Material gradually lose their special properties over months to years; enchantments may fade, foods stop being magically nutritious.
- **Shadowfell:** Feywild and Shadowfell goods are thematic opposites. Shadowfell merchants will pay premium for Feywild goods (and vice versa) because neither can easily source them domestically.

## II.4 — THE INNER PLANES

The Inner Planes are the building blocks of physical reality — vast, hostile, and economically defined by what they produce rather than what they trade. Commerce here is not about culture or ideology (as on the Outer Planes) but about extracting and moving the fundamental materials of existence. The Inner Planes are the multiverse's industrial zones.

**General rules for Inner Plane commerce:**
- Hard currencies (especially jinx) are accepted at major trade nodes and by established merchants; barter dominates in the deep interior
- Elemental creatures are not inherently commercial; many are indifferent to mortal wealth and must be approached with appropriate trade goods
- The archomentals (the elemental lords, good and evil) control the major trade routes on their respective planes and take tribute from traffic
- Environmental protection is mandatory and expensive — without it, you'll die before you can spend anything

### II.4.1 — THE PLANE OF AIR
*The Boundless Sky*

The Plane of Air is an infinite three-dimensional atmosphere — no ground, no ceiling, no walls. The wind is constant, from gentle breezes to devastating storms. Visibility varies; clouds can be dense enough to lose yourself in for days. The inhabitants are air elementals, djinn, aerial creatures, and the Aaqa — a city of cloud and wind maintained by the djinni as the plane's primary trading hub.

**Economic Character:**
The Plane of Air rewards mobility. Merchants who can fly (or who ride aerial mounts) have enormous advantages; those who cannot are dependent on the djinni's courtesy. The djinni are proud, generous, and intensely aware of their social standing — a deal struck through proper respect yields better prices than the same deal struck through rudeness.

The plane's primary exports are wind-aspected goods, storm materials, and the creative work of the djinni, who are among the finest artisans in the multiverse when they choose to be. ## II.4.6 — THE QUASI-ELEMENTAL PLANES

The Quasi-Elemental Planes form at the borders between the four elemental planes and the two energy planes (Positive and Negative). There are eight of them. Four touch the Positive Energy Plane, combining elemental substance with raw life-force; four touch the Negative Energy Plane, combining elemental substance with entropy. They are the strangest of the Inner Planes: frontier zones, rarely visited, where extraordinary materials can be found by those willing to risk the environments.

**General note:** None of the quasi-elemental planes have stable merchant populations. Commerce here is barter and hard currency. The prices listed are what these goods sell for at their borders or in Sigil.

## II.4.7 — THE POSITIVE ENERGY PLANE

The Positive Energy Plane is not a place mortals go willingly. It is a plane of pure, raw, life-affirming energy — so much so that it overwhelms most forms of life rather than sustaining them. A mortal entering without protection experiences profound euphoria, followed within minutes by a cascading overload of their life force, followed by transformation into a being of pure energy (which, from the perspective of the mortal who just ceased to exist, is effectively death).

**Economic Character:**
The Positive Energy Plane hosts almost no commerce. What exists happens at the plane's borders or through specialized entities — powerful celestials, high-CR elementals, and the rare mortal spellcaster with sufficient protection to spend hours (not days) in the plane's terrible abundance. The plane's "products" are correspondingly extreme.

## II.4.8 — THE NEGATIVE ENERGY PLANE

The Negative Energy Plane is the Positive Energy Plane's opposite — a plane of pure, raw, life-devouring energy. Undeath made environment. A mortal entering without protection feels their life force being pulled from their body; within minutes, they are dead, and unless specifically warded, their corpse rises as undead.

**Economic Character:**
Similar to the Positive Energy Plane, the Negative Energy Plane hosts minimal commerce. The **Doomguard** maintains a significant presence here (the plane aligns with their philosophy of entropy). Undead of various kinds dwell here, though most are not traders in the conventional sense.

## II.5 — THE OUTER PLANES

The Outer Planes are the realms of belief, ideology, and alignment made manifest. If the Inner Planes are foundations of physics, the Outer Planes are foundations of meaning. Here, economies are not shaped by scarcity or environmental hostility but by the philosophy of the plane itself. Mount Celestia's economy runs on bureaucratic virtue; Baator's on contractual rapacity; Limbo's on chaos literalized.

For the PC who reaches level 5+ and begins plane-hopping seriously, the Outer Planes provide the richest and strangest economic experiences in the multiverse. They are also where the greatest rewards and the greatest risks lie. A fair deal on Mount Celestia might bless your weapons. A fair deal in Baator might cost your soul.

---

### II.5.1 — THE SEVEN MOUNTING HEAVENS OF CELESTIA (Lawful Good)
*The Paragon of Ordered Righteousness*

Mount Celestia is a great mountain with seven ascending layers, each more blessed than the last. It is ruled (as much as that term applies) by the archons — a celestial race who have organized the mountain's affairs into the most orderly bureaucracy in the multiverse.

**Economic Character:**
The Celestian economy is defined by three principles: fairness, documentation, and patience. Prices are fixed — haggling is not just frowned upon but culturally offensive, implying the initial price was unjust. Every transaction is documented. Getting anything substantial done takes time — archon bureaucracy is thorough, not rapid.

The plane's "exports" are primarily services: healing, blessing, crafting of holy items, spiritual counsel, and arbitration of conflicts.

#### Celestia Currencies

| Currency | Value | Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Celestian Trade Bars | See II.1.3 | Primary formal currency | The backbone of Celestian commerce |
| Merit Tokens | Variable | Non-transferable tokens earned through righteous action | A spiritual credit score; cannot be transferred; spent on access to higher-tier services |
| Archon Writs | 50–500 gp equivalent | Official documents authorizing specific purchases or services | The writ system is how Celestia handles administrative commerce |
| Ephel-Silver | 1 ES = 2 gp | Sanctified silver from the higher layers (Solania and above) | Premium currency within Celestia; items radiate holy auras |
| Throne-Gold | 100 gp per coin | Gold minted on Chronias, the highest layer; issued in extremely limited quantities | Institutional currency for treaty-level transactions; a PC who possesses one is marked as significant |
| Crown of Chronias | 100,000+ gp | Theoretical ceremonial object serving as currency for extreme transactions | Perhaps a dozen exist; when one changes hands, it shapes planar politics |

**Storage and Wealth Management:**
The great Archon Archives in the highest layer serve as the plane's central banking system. For outsiders, the **Silver Bastion** in the second layer (Mercuria) provides more accessible banking — egalitarian but bound by Celestian documentation standards.

**Economic Friction:**
Lawful good characters find everything easier on Celestia: discounts, priority service, ready assistance. Chaotic evil characters find everything harder: prices inflated, paperwork multiplied, service perpetually delayed. The archons are not deliberately punitive — the plane's alignment permeates its institutions.

### II.5.2 — BYTOPIA (Lawful Good / Neutral Good)
*The Twin Paradises of Productive Labor*

Bytopia consists of two planes stacked "ceiling to ceiling" — Dothion (the settled upper lands) and Shurrock (the wilderness below). It is a plane of farms, workshops, small towns, and pastoral prosperity, all of which demand honest work from their inhabitants.

**Economic Character:**
Bytopia's economy is the closest thing the Upper Planes have to "normal" commerce. Markets operate. Prices fluctuate modestly. The plane specializes in *excellence* — not miraculous excellence, but reliable excellence. A Bytopian blade is exactly as sharp as promised.

#### Bytopia Currencies

**Storage:** The **Gnomish Commercial Cooperatives** — a loose federation of locally-chartered banks across Dothion and Shurrock — provide deposits, interest-bearing accounts (2–3% annual), letters of credit valid throughout the Upper Planes, and investment services.

**Economic Character:**
The gift economy functions alongside the formal commerce necessary for visitors. An Elysian who gives you food expects nothing in return — but trusts you to give something to someone else who needs it. The gift multiplies outward.

### II.5.8 — MECHANUS (Lawful Neutral)
*The Great Machine of Perfect Order*

Mechanus is the plane of absolute law — a realm of vast interlocking gears turning in perfect synchronization across an infinite three-dimensional lattice. The modrons execute their functions with mathematical precision.

Mechanus has the most stable economy in the multiverse. Every price is fixed. Every contract is binding and automatically enforced by the plane's nature. No haggling. No fraud (Bluff checks take –10; the plane suppresses dishonesty). No credit unless documented.

### II.5.10 — THE NINE HELLS OF BAATOR (Lawful Evil)
*Hell in Nine Layers*

Baator is the plane of lawful evil — a realm where law is wielded as the ultimate weapon, where contracts are destiny, and where the devils have turned the machinery of order into a perfect system for domination and suffering. The nine layers descend from Avernus (the war-front against the demons) to Nessus (the Pit, where Asmodeus rules from his palace of lies).

**Economic Character:**
Baator's economy is the most sophisticated evil economy in the multiverse. It is hierarchical, legalistic, and absolutely ruthless. The currency of ultimate worth is souls — specifically the souls of mortals, bound through the infernal contract system. But mortal commerce also functions, regulated by the pit lords who control each layer.

#### Baator Currencies

## II.6 — METAPHYSICAL CURRENCIES

These currencies are not material objects — they are crystallized aspects of reality itself. They are the most powerful, most valuable, and most dangerous mediums of exchange in the multiverse. Buying something with a soul shell is not "using a rare coin." It is spending something that was once a person.

PCs encountering metaphysical currencies for the first time — usually around levels 5–7 — should feel the weight of what they're handling. These are not just valuable items. They are ontological objects that raise questions about consciousness, morality, and reality itself.

---

### II.6.1 — SOUL SHELLS
*The Most Controversial Currency in Existence*

#### What They Are

When a petitioner — the post-mortem spiritual residue of a mortal — is "harvested" (killed, consumed, or drained on the Outer Planes), the remaining essence can be crystallized into a small, translucent shell approximately the size and shape of a robin's egg. This is a **soul shell**. It contains the distilled spiritual energy of a being that once lived, thought, loved, and feared.

#### Grading System

Soul shells are graded by the night hag soul-brokers who dominate the trade:

| Grade | Source | Value (gp) | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dross | Weak petitioner (commoner, low-wisdom mortal) | 10–50 gp | Cloudy, small, faint glow; used in bulk for low-grade magical fuel |
| Common | Average petitioner | 50–200 gp | Clear, steady glow; standard trade unit |
| Choice | Strong-willed petitioner (classed NPC, devout worshipper) | 200–1,000 gp | Bright, warm glow; prized for enchantment and binding rituals |
| Prime | Exceptional petitioner (heroic mortal, powerful caster, saint) | 1,000–5,000 gp | Brilliant, almost painful to look at; sings faintly; used in artifact creation |
| Singular | Unique petitioner (legendary hero, demigod remnant, fallen angel) | 5,000–50,000 gp | Each is unique; named; has properties reflecting the original soul's nature |
| Paragon | Legendary hero, demigod-touched, exceptional caster of cosmic influence | 50,000–250,000 gp | Quest-tier items; each is a named artifact-equivalent |
| Mythic | True demigod-aspect soul, or mortal who achieved transcendence before harvest | 250,000–2,000,000+ gp | Multi-campaign-defining; their existence is rumor in the Lower Planes' highest circles |

#### The Soul Trade

The soul trade is the dark engine of the Lower Planes' economy. Night hags are the primary brokers:
1. **Harvesting:** Petitioners are captured on the Outer Planes and drained. This process destroys the petitioner permanently — they do not reform.
2. **Processing:** Raw soul-essence is condensed into shell form through a ritual known only to night hags and certain powerful fiends.
3. **Grading:** Assessed through magical appraisal. Grading soul shells requires Knowledge (planes) 10 ranks; base DC 22.
4. **Sale:** Sold in Grey Waste towns (particularly Oinos), Gehenna's forge-cities, Baator's open markets (particularly Dis and Minauros), and through discrete brokers in Sigil's Hive Ward.

#### Moral Weight

This is not morally neutral. Trading in soul shells is:
- An **evil act** in PF1e alignment terms: buying or selling a soul shell shifts a character's alignment one step toward evil (per transaction; max one shift per level)
- **Illegal** in Sigil under Harmonium regulations, though Hive Ward enforcement is inconsistent
- **Illegal and violently punished** on all Upper Planes
- **Legal and expected** on most Lower Planes
- **Legal but stigmatized** in the Outlands (gate-town dependent)

**GM Guidance:** Never let soul shell trading become routine or comfortable. If a player begins treating souls as "just another commodity," the game world should push back.

### II.6.2 — BELIEF TOKENS
*Faith Made Tangible*

#### What They Are

On the Outer Planes, belief has literal, physical power. Belief Tokens are crystallized fragments of that conviction — small, faceted crystals that glow with a color corresponding to their alignment and philosophical content. They don't contain a specific belief ("Pelor is the one true sun god") — they contain *conviction itself*, the raw force of a mortal caring deeply about something.

#### Formation

Belief Tokens form naturally at locations of intense faith: temples, shrines, sites of martyrdom. They can also be manufactured by:
- Clerics and oracles (CL 7+) through a 4-hour meditation ritual (produces 1d4 tokens of the caster's alignment, each worth 10–50 gp)
- Proxy servants of powers as part of their divine duties
- Philosophical institutions (particularly the Believers of the Source and the Sign of One) through collective meditation

#### Valuation

| Token Alignment | Value on Same-Aligned Plane | Value on Opposed Plane | Value in Sigil |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lawful Good | 25–50 gp | 5 gp (curiosity) or refused | 15–30 gp |
| Chaotic Good | 20–40 gp | 5 gp or refused | 10–25 gp |
| Lawful Evil | 20–45 gp | 5 gp or actively destroyed | 10–25 gp |
| Chaotic Evil | 15–35 gp | 5 gp or actively destroyed | 10–20 gp |
| True Neutral | 30–60 gp on any Neutral plane | 10–15 gp elsewhere | 20–40 gp |

**The True Neutral premium:** Neutral Belief Tokens are the most universally valued because they don't trigger alignment-based rejection. The Rilmani of the Outlands and the Athar of Sigil are both major buyers.

#### Uses

1. **Planar Magic Fuel:** Can substitute for material components in planar magic at a 2:1 gp-value ratio.
2. **Petitioner Interaction:** Offering an appropriate token grants +4 on Diplomacy checks with that petitioner.
3. **Faction Currency:** Several Sigil factions accept Belief Tokens directly (Believers of the Source accept any alignment; the Athar accept only Neutral tokens; the Harmonium accept LG and LN tokens as supplementary payment for permits).
4. **Divine Offering:** Presenting Belief Tokens at a deity's temple increases the value of the offering by 50% for purposes of divine-interaction effects.

### II.6.3 — MEMORY PEARLS
*Bottled Experience*

#### What They Are

Memory Pearls are small, opalescent spheres (about pea-sized) that contain a single, complete memory extracted from a sentient creature. The memory is moved, not copied — the original creature no longer possesses it. The pearl can be "read" by holding it against the temple and concentrating (standard action), playing the memory as a vivid sensory experience. The memory remains in the pearl after reading; it can be read multiple times.

#### Creation

- **Psionic extraction** (psions, wilders, or psychic creatures with appropriate abilities)
- **Arcane extraction** (*modify memory* + a 200 gp pearl + 1 hour ritual, CL 9+)
- **Night hag dream-harvesting** (during Nightmare Haunting ability)
- **Natural formation on the Astral Plane** (memories of dead gods occasionally crystallize spontaneously)

#### Valuation

| Memory Type | Typical Value | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Mundane personal memory | 1–10 gp | A childhood birthday, a sunset, a first kiss |
| Skilled knowledge | 50–500 gp | How to pick a specific lock; the layout of a building |
| Emotional experience (intense) | 25–200 gp | The Society of Sensation pays premium for novel experiences |
| Secret or intelligence | 100–10,000+ gp | A password; a hidden weakness; a political secret |
| Magical knowledge | 500–5,000+ gp | Somatic components of a rare spell; location of a hidden demiplane |
| Historical record | 50–5,000 gp | Eyewitness memory of a significant event |
| Memory of a dead god | 10,000–100,000+ gp | Astral-harvested; contains fragments of divine knowledge |
| Memory of a living god's private moment | 100,000–1,000,000+ gp | Extraordinarily rare; possession of one is a cosmic-political event |
| Memory of a pivotal cosmic event | 1,000,000+ gp | A deity's ascension, a realm's creation, a treaty between powers |
| The dying memory of a power | Priceless; Senate-of-powers artifact tier | A Memory Pearl formed at the moment of a deity's death; only a handful theorized to exist |

#### The Society of Sensation Connection

The Sensates are the largest institutional buyer. Sensate Purchase Rates:
- Novel sensory experience (not in Sensorium): Base value + 50%
- Duplicate experience: Base value – 50%
- Dangerous/extreme experience: Base value + 100%
- Non-humanoid perspective (dragon, elemental, tree): Base value + 200%

### II.6.4 — CHAOS SHARDS
*Crystallized Possibility*

Chaos Shards are fragments of Limbo's ever-shifting matter, temporarily stabilized. They appear as irregular crystals of shifting, iridescent color.

#### The Volatility Mechanic

**Base Value:** When first acquired, establish base value: 1d6 × 10 gp (range 10–60 gp per shard).

**Transaction Volatility:** Every time a Chaos Shard changes hands, roll 1d6:

| Roll | Multiplier | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ×0 | The shard destabilizes and becomes worthless mud/vapor. Gone. |
| 2 | ×0.5 | Half base value. The shard is flickering, unstable. |
| 3–4 | ×1.0 | Base value. Normal. |
| 5 | ×1.5 | The shard is vibrant, pulsing with potential. Above-average value. |
| 6 | ×2.0 | The shard has momentarily crystallized into something remarkable. Double value. |

**Stabilization:** *Order's Wrath*, a githzerai monk's Ki stabilization (DC 20 concentration check, 1 minute), or containment in a lead-lined Mechanus-forged box can lock a Chaos Shard's value at its current roll for 24 hours.

**Who Accepts Them:**
- Limbo: Standard currency; everyone accepts the volatility
- Sigil: Specialist dealers and Xaositects only; most merchants refuse
- Mechanus: Absolutely not. A modron would sooner swallow a gear.
- Other Planes: Accepted only by planar traders willing to gamble

### II.6.5 — TRUE NAMES
*The Ultimate Currency*

#### What They Are

A True Name is the fundamental, metaphysical identity of a being — spoken with knowledge and intent, it gives the speaker power over the named creature. True Names exist for outsiders, for some powerful mortal casters, and theoretically for powers themselves. Each transaction involving a True Name is unique, high-stakes, and often deadly.

#### Valuation

| True Name Of | Approximate Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Minor outsider (CR 1–5) | 500–2,000 gp | Useful for binding and summoning |
| Moderate outsider (CR 6–10) | 2,000–10,000 gp | Significantly powerful bindings |
| Major outsider (CR 11–15) | 10,000–50,000 gp | Dangerous to possess; the named creature will hunt you |
| Greater outsider (CR 16–20) | 50,000–200,000 gp | Nation-toppling power; attracts attention from every faction |
| Named fiend lord or celestial paragon | 200,000–2,000,000 gp | Not merely sold; leveraged. Possession is a campaign-defining event. |
| Archdevil, Demon Lord, Archangel | 2,000,000–20,000,000+ gp | Rarely sold outright; held by factions and leveraged against each other |
| A Greater Power | Not tradeable | Theoretical; possession alone is dangerous beyond description |
| An Overgod or cosmic entity | The universe does not have a price | Such a name is not currency. It is the end of a setting. |

#### The Danger

Possessing a True Name is like carrying a live explosive. The named creature knows its name is in circulation (DC 25 Will save when the name is spoken or written) and will take steps to recover it. True Names are traded in absolute secrecy, using dead drops, coded language, and *Modify Memory* to protect the chain of custody. The Fraternity of Order maintains a classified registry of known True Names — access to which is one of the most jealously guarded privileges in Sigil.

### II.6.6 — FAVORS & DEBTS
*The Currency That Runs Everything*

Beneath every formal currency system in the multiverse is the economy of favors and debts. "I owe you one" is the most universal medium of exchange — it works on every plane, in every culture, and at every level of play.

#### Favor Magnitude Scale

| Level | Name | Approximate gp Equivalent | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Courtesy | 10–50 gp | Pass a message; make an introduction; lend a tool |
| 2 | Service | 50–500 gp | Provide shelter; share information; cover someone's tab |
| 3 | Sacrifice | 500–5,000 gp | Risk danger on someone's behalf; use significant resources; call in your own favor |
| 4 | Blood Debt | 5,000–50,000 gp | Save a life; sacrifice something precious; take on serious personal risk |
| 5 | Life Debt | 50,000+ gp | Save someone from permanent destruction; restore a shattered reputation; return a stolen soul |

#### Key Rules

- Favors cannot be precisely converted to gp; the equivalent is a guideline
- A favor owed to a faction counts as standing; completing a Level 3 favor for the Harmonium is equivalent to a significant standing increase
- **On Baator:** Favors are contracts and are supernaturally binding; failing to repay triggers contractual consequences
- **On Celestia:** Favors are sacred obligations tracked by the archon bureaucracy; failing to repay damages your Merit Token record
- **In the Abyss:** Favors are meaningless unless enforced by immediate threat of violence
- **In the Feywild:** Favors can become Fey Bargains if accepted in specific ways; extreme caution warranted
- **On Carceri:** Favors can become physical chains

### II.6.7 — DIVINE MARKS AND CELESTIAL COMMISSIONS
*The Economy of the Gods*

For PCs operating at level 15+, a new category of metaphysical currency becomes relevant: direct economic interaction with divine beings.

**Divine Marks:** A formal acknowledgment from a deity that a specific being has their favor. Not transferable; cannot be spent directly. But a PC bearing a Divine Mark of Pelor can expect:
- Discounts at all Pelor-aligned institutions (10–30%)
- Access to services normally requiring clerical status
- Immunity from lower-tier divine servants who recognize the mark
- Complications with opposed deities who see it

**Divine Commission (value 50,000–500,000 gp):** A direct request from a deity for a specific service. Completion earns the equivalent value in divine blessings, which translate to mechanical benefits (GM's discretion) and social standing with the deity's portfolio.

**Intercession Request (value 10,000+ gp):** A formal request that a deity speak on your behalf to another power. Requires divine relationship; equivalent to an extremely high-level favor.

### II.7.3 — Three-Question Conversion Protocol

When a PC wants to buy something in an unfamiliar plane, answer three questions:

1. **What currency do they have?** (If jinx in Sigil → no friction, done.)
2. **What currency does the seller want?** (Match → done. Mismatch → apply friction from II.7.1.)
3. **What plane?** (Apply PPM from Module III. That's the price adjustment for the item itself.)

If all three have answers, you have a complete transaction. If any is ambiguous, default to: "apply 10% friction and continue."

## II.8 — THE WEALTH SPECTRUM: FROM DESTITUTION TO COSMIC DOMINION

*What does "rich" mean across the multiverse? This section defines the full wealth spectrum — twelve tiers from the destitute to the genuinely cosmic. Tiers 1–8 are the PC wealth range (tracking closely to PF1e WBL). Tiers 9–12 are narrative constructs: kings, faction leaders, demon lords, and the ultimate planar magnates. Every tier includes what you can buy, how you store your wealth, what economic activities you can conduct, and what power your wealth represents.*

### Understanding the Tiers

| Tier | Name | Net Worth (gp) | Approximate PF1e Level | Who Occupies This Tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Destitute | 0–9 gp | N/A | Beggars; newly freed slaves; complete paupers |
| 1 | Struggling | 10–149 gp | Level 1 | Day laborers; beginning apprentices; rural poor |
| 2 | Working | 150–999 gp | Levels 1–2 | Skilled craftsmen; minor merchants; town guards |
| 3 | Comfortable | 1,000–5,999 gp | Levels 3–5 | Established merchants; junior adventurers; minor officials |
| 4 | Prosperous | 6,000–15,999 gp | Levels 5–7 | Successful merchants; mid-level adventurers; senior officers |
| 5 | Wealthy | 16,000–35,999 gp | Levels 7–9 | Wealthy merchants; experienced adventurers; minor nobles |
| 6 | Very Wealthy | 36,000–100,000 gp | Levels 9–12 | Major merchants; senior adventurers; lesser nobility |
| 7 | Rich | 100,001–300,000 gp | Levels 12–15 | Merchant princes; experienced adventurers; major nobles; faction leaders |
| 8 | Very Rich | 300,001–760,000 gp | Levels 15–20 | WBL cap zone; powerful adventurers; senior faction leadership |
| 9 | Ultra-Wealthy | 760,001–5,000,000 gp | Beyond WBL | City rulers; major daemon lords; senior archdevils; legendary merchants |
| 10 | Magnate | 5,000,001–50,000,000 gp | Narrative only | Planar lords; nation-scale wealth; significant faction control |
| 11 | Planar Power | 50,000,001–500,000,000 gp | Narrative only | Demon princes; archdevil hierarchs; planar merchant empires |
| 12 | Cosmic Wealth | 500,000,001+ gp | Narrative only | Greater powers; the Lady of Pain's implicit treasury; the oldest and most powerful entities |

### TIER 0 — DESTITUTE (0–9 gp)

**What this looks like:** Nothing. Perhaps the clothes on your back, a bowl for begging, and whatever you can scavenge. On Sigil, this means the Hive — sleeping in alleys, eating whatever the Sensates' charitable kitchens provide. On Athas, it means dying slowly of thirst. On the Grey Waste, it means you're already a petitioner and don't quite remember having had more.

**Economic Activities:**
- Begging (1d6 cp per day in a generous city; nothing in most places)
- Day labor for food (no pay; just survival)
- Scavenging
- Desperate barter of personal possessions

**Storage:** Your pockets. You have nothing to store.

**Sigil specifics:** The Revolutionary League maintains soup-kitchens and safe-houses that serve Tier 0 individuals without charge. The Bleak Cabal does likewise, out of a grim sense of obligation. The Fated consider Tier 0 individuals to be failing at the fundamental task of existence and refuse charity.

---

### TIER 1 — STRUGGLING (10–149 gp)
*Approximate PF1e Level 1 Starting Wealth*

**What this looks like:** You have a trade. You have food most days. You own one good set of clothes. You live in rented rooms. You are one bad month from Tier 0.

**What You Can Buy:**
- Full standard equipment kit: weapons, armor, adventuring gear (~50–150 gp)
- A week's comfortable room and board in a decent city inn (1 gp/day)
- Basic spell components and minor consumables (healing potions, alchemist's fire, etc.)
- Hireling for a day or a week
- Passage on a merchant vessel for a short journey

**Storage:** A lockbox or hidden cache. Most Tier 1 wealth is carried.

**Currency Used:** Copper and silver predominantly. A gold piece is a significant event.

---

### TIER 2 — WORKING (150–999 gp)
*Approximate PF1e Levels 1–2*

**What this looks like:** A skilled craftsman at the peak of their trade. You own your tools. You might own your workplace. You have savings.

**What You Can Buy:**
- Light armor of any standard type
- A horse and tack
- A week's private room at a nice inn
- A minor magic item (potion of cure light wounds, universal solvent, etc.)
- Hire a short-term skilled specialist (a scribe, a tracker, a guide)
- One-time use of a portal in the Outlands (typically 1–5 gp per person)

**Storage:** A personal safe; deposit with a temple bank; letters of credit from a merchant house (at this tier: sometimes, if you have an account).

**Currency Used:** Primarily silver and gold. Occasional platinum for large transactions.

---

### TIER 3 — COMFORTABLE (1,000–5,999 gp)
*Approximate PF1e Levels 3–5*

**What this looks like:** A successful junior adventurer or an established merchant. You have gear that matters. You have reserves. You can afford to make mistakes.

**What You Can Buy:**
- Most standard magic items (rings of protection +1, cloaks of resistance, etc.)
- A small boat
- Hire a full party of skilled hirelings for a week
- Commission a masterwork weapon or armor
- A modest city property (a room, a small shop)
- Brief planar travel (a portal trip to the Outlands and back, with expenses)

**Storage:** Temple banking or merchant-house letters of credit become practical. A Consortium Vault Account is not yet available (minimum 10,000 jinx).

**Currency Used:** Gold predominantly; occasional platinum; first encounters with Trade Bars.

---

### TIER 4 — PROSPEROUS (6,000–15,999 gp)
*Approximate PF1e Levels 5–7*

**What this looks like:** A mid-level adventuring party with gear. A merchant who runs a profitable business. A noble's second son with an inheritance. In Sigil, you're the person the Hive Ward knows is worth following home.

**What You Can Buy:**
- Most magic items in the 5,000–8,000 gp range
- An established business property
- Maintain a small household staff (5–10 people)
- Commission planar information from the Fraternity of Order
- Hire a skilled specialist for a month (a wizard, a ranger, a scout)
- A brief guided expedition to the Outlands or a stable Outer Plane

**Storage:** Consortium Vault Account becomes available (minimum 10,000 jinx). Temple banking at established institutions.

**WBL Note:** A 5th-level PC has ~10,500 gp in equipment value. This tier captures what PC gear "costs" at the lower-middle levels.

---

### TIER 5 — WEALTHY (16,000–35,999 gp)
*Approximate PF1e Levels 7–9*

**What this looks like:** A successful adventuring party. A major city merchant. A temple's operating funds. In Sigil, someone the Consortium's junior staff recognize by face.

**What You Can Buy:**
- Magic items in the 10,000–20,000 gp range (rings of protection +3, amulets of natural armor +3, etc.)
- A modest city estate (a house with grounds)
- Hire a team of experts for a multi-week project
- Access to Consortium specialty services
- Basic faction-rank advancement (Outlands contacts, junior faction membership fees)
- A planar expedition with guide and supplies

**Storage:** Consortium Vault Account, earning 1–3% annual interest. Letters of credit accepted nearly anywhere in the planes.

**Planar Integration:** At this tier, PCs traveling to Sigil or the Outlands for the first time will feel wealthy relative to the plane's common folk but not relative to established planar traders.

---

### TIER 6 — VERY WEALTHY (36,000–100,000 gp)
*Approximate PF1e Levels 9–12*

**What this looks like:** A major faction member's personal reserves. A lesser planar lord's operating budget. A successful planar merchant who has made multiple profitable runs.

**What You Can Buy:**
- Magic items in the 30,000–60,000 gp range (headbands of vast intelligence, belts of giant strength, major rings)
- A significant property (a planar estate, a small keep)
- Hire a team of high-level specialists (including casters of level 7+)
- Commission research from the Fraternity of Order at their highest public tiers
- Minor faction leadership fees
- Funding an expedition to a hostile elemental or outer plane with professional support

**Storage:** Consortium Private Banking threshold approaches (1,000,000 gp for full private banking, but accounts in this range receive preliminary premium treatment). Vault Ingots become a practical denomination.

**Planar Footprint:** At this tier, your name begins to matter in Sigil's commercial circles. You might be remembered at specific stalls in the Great Bazaar.

---

### TIER 7 — RICH (100,001–300,000 gp)
*Approximate PF1e Levels 12–15*

**What this looks like:** A senior faction member. A major Outlands merchant house. A lesser archon with accumulated resources. In the Lower Planes, a significant yugoloth contract holder.

**What You Can Buy:**
- The highest practical magic items (artifacts not included; highest standard items cap around 200,000 gp)
- A substantial real property (a tower, a planar waystation, a small keep with staff)
- Maintain a personal staff of 20–50 people
- Fund a small-scale intelligence operation (6 months of information gathering, 5–10 agents)
- Commission major magical research (CL 15+)
- A personal planar portal (to one specific plane) purchased through the Fraternity of Order (100,000–300,000 gp depending on stability and reliability)

**Storage:** Consortium Private Banking (available at 1,000,000 gp account value; approaching in this tier). Vault Ingots. Letters of credit at the highest institutional rates.

---

### TIER 8 — VERY RICH (300,001–760,000 gp)
*Approximate PF1e Level 20 (WBL Cap Zone)*

**What this looks like:** A 20th-level adventurer fully equipped. A senior planar merchant with established trade routes. A major faction's treasury officer. The lower edge of genuine power.

**What You Can Buy:**
- The most powerful standard magic items
- A multi-building compound or planar estate with full staff (50–100 people)
- Maintain a personal intelligence network covering a single plane (10–20 agents)
- Fund an extended military operation (a company of mercenaries for 3 months)
- Commission artifact-adjacent items (approaching artifact tier, not artifacts themselves)
- Major planar portal installation (permanent, two-plane, full-reliability)
- A small merchant fleet (3–5 vessels)

**WBL Note:** A 20th-level PC has approximately 760,000 gp in total equipment value. This is the top of the PC wealth curve. Everything above is narrative territory.

**Storage:** Full Consortium Private Banking. Multiple Vault Ingots. Foundry Monolith-level accounts. Consortium portfolio investments.

---

### TIER 9 — ULTRA-WEALTHY (760,001–5,000,000 gp)
*Beyond WBL — Narrative Construct*

At this point, wealth has transcended individual purchasing power and become institutional. The ultra-wealthy don't spend their money — they *operate* it. Their wealth is infrastructure: trade routes, properties, organizations, political relationships.

**Who Is Here:**
- City rulers of significant cities
- Senior faction leaders (Factol-level)
- Minor planar lords (the lord of a single Outlands gate-town, for example)
- The wealthiest mortal merchants in Sigil
- Lesser yugoloth generals
- Senior archdevils of lower-ranking layers

**What Ultra-Wealth Enables:**

*Personal Services:*
- Full-time bodyguard detail of high-level fighters or casters (6–12 individuals, 20,000–50,000 gp/year)
- Personal advisor staff (spymaster, legal counsel, diplomatic representative, trade director: 30,000–80,000 gp/year total)
- A private portal to Sigil from wherever you call home

*Intelligence Networks:*
- A single-plane intelligence network: 15–30 agents, cover identities, safe-houses, communication infrastructure. Cost: 100,000–250,000 gp to establish; 30,000–75,000 gp/year to maintain.
- Information from: significant political events, notable commerce movements, personality profiles of relevant figures, advance warning of threats
- A multi-plane intelligence network (3–5 planes): 500,000–2,000,000 gp to establish; 150,000–400,000 gp/year to maintain

*Military Capability:*
- A company of high-quality mercenaries (100 fighters, level 5–8): 50,000 gp/month active service; 10,000 gp/month retainer
- A small private army (500 troops, mixed): 200,000 gp/month active; 40,000 gp/month retainer
- Commissioning specialist units: Yugoloth platoon (CR 10+): 15,000 gp/operation; Celestial honor guard (if alignment permits): 10,000 gp/month; Elemental company (if faction stands permit): 20,000 gp/month

*Property and Infrastructure:*
- A planar waystation (a secure building in a specific plane, with portal access): 200,000–500,000 gp
- A private demiplane (small; stable; requires a CL 20 caster + materials): 1,000,000+ gp
- A fully staffed merchant complex in Sigil (10 stalls, warehouse, private office): 300,000–500,000 gp

*Banking and Investment:*
- Full Consortium Private Banking with investment portfolio
- Consortium portfolio returns: 3–8% annually on managed funds
- Demiplane bond investment (backing stable demiplane creation projects): 8–12% annually (higher risk)

**Storage:** Foundry Monolith. Consortium portfolio. Real property. The ultra-wealthy hold most of their wealth in illiquid assets (properties, relationships, routes) rather than coins.

---

### TIER 10 — MAGNATE (5,000,001–50,000,000 gp)
*City-Scale Wealth*

**Who Is Here:**
- Rulers of large cities or small kingdoms
- Archdevils of significant layers (Mammon, Dispater, etc.)
- The Oinoloth (yugoloth general leadership)
- Faction leaders at the peak of major Sigil factions
- Major elemental lords
- Senior planar merchant empires with dozens of trade routes

**What Magnate-Wealth Enables:**

*Military:*
- A full mercenary army (5,000 troops, mixed levels): 2,000,000 gp to raise; 500,000 gp/month maintenance
- Specialized forces: a company of elite githyanki (500): 1,000,000 gp/year; a yugoloth battalion: 2,000,000 gp/operation
- Naval power (if relevant plane): 10 warships crewed and maintained: 500,000 gp/year
- A standing defense force around a specific location: 200,000–1,000,000 gp/year

*Intelligence:*
- A multi-plane intelligence empire covering 10+ planes: 1,000,000 gp/year
- Deep penetration operations (long-term agents in enemy institutions): 100,000–500,000 gp per placement
- Disinformation campaigns (shaping political events across multiple planes): 500,000–2,000,000 gp per major operation

*Political Power:*
- Funding a Sigil faction's major operation: 500,000–5,000,000 gp
- Buying political influence in a major gate-town government: 1,000,000–10,000,000 gp
- Establishing a new trade route (infrastructure, security, portals): 2,000,000–20,000,000 gp

*Commerce:*
- A planar trade empire: 20–50 trade routes, warehouses on 5+ planes, 500+ employees: generating 500,000–5,000,000 gp/year revenue
- A private bank (smaller than the Consortium; operates in a specific zone): 5,000,000–10,000,000 gp to establish

---

### TIER 11 — PLANAR POWER (50,000,001–500,000,000 gp)
*Plane-Scale Wealth*

**Who Is Here:**
- Major demon lords (Demogorgon, Orcus, etc.)
- Archdevils of the highest ranks (Baalzebul, Mephistopheles, Glasya)
- The ruling council of major planar powers
- The wealthiest entities in the City of Brass (the Sultan himself)
- Senior archomentals at peak power

**What Planar Power-Wealth Enables:**

*Military:*
- An army of 50,000+ creatures maintained indefinitely
- Multiple specialist corps including casters, cavalry, and siege assets
- The ability to project military power across multiple planes simultaneously
- Purchasing the services of the Dark Eight (yugoloth generals) for a major campaign: 10,000,000–50,000,000 gp

*Intelligence and Information:*
- A multiverse-spanning intelligence network covering every significant plane
- Near-complete information about significant events within your sphere of influence
- Agents embedded in every major faction, government, and commercial institution

*Political Power:*
- The ability to reshape a faction's policy through economic leverage
- Direct control of multiple gate-towns through property and debt
- Influence over planar weather, resource allocation, and trade routes

*Unique Capabilities:*
- Commissioning entirely new demiplanes at scale (full-sized, stable, self-sustaining): 50,000,000+ gp
- Warding an entire plane against specific threats: 10,000,000–100,000,000 gp
- Funding a divine portfolio's expansion: backing mortals in religious wars; funding temples; seeding new faith populations

---

### TIER 12 — COSMIC WEALTH (500,000,001+ gp)
*Reality-Scale Wealth*

**Who Is Here:**
- Greater powers (CR 30+, the actual gods of the multiverse)
- The Lady of Pain (implicit; her wealth is the wealth of Sigil itself)
- Asmodeus's ninth-layer treasury
- The most ancient beings in the multiverse

At this tier, the concept of "wealth" as discrete units of currency becomes a category error. These entities don't have bank accounts — they have planes. They don't spend money — they move resources. Their "wealth" is the capacity to reshape reality, redirect cosmic forces, or simply do what lesser beings must pay for.

**For GM Reference Only:**

A greater power's annual "revenue" in divine worship, territorial taxation, and cosmic tribute might be in the range of 10,000,000,000–100,000,000,000 gp equivalent. These numbers are not for PCs to access, arbitrage, or meaningfully interact with except as forces of nature.

The only PC interaction with Tier 12 wealth is as supplicants, adventurers seeking boons, or (at the very highest levels) as individuals who have done something so significant that a Tier 12 entity regards them as worth noticing.

## II.9 — MONEY-CHANGERS AND WHERE TO FIND THEM

Knowing what currencies exist is half the problem. The other half is knowing where to exchange them.

### II.9.1 — THE INFINITE COIN (Sigil, Great Bazaar)

The Merkhant Consortium's flagship exchange office. Housed in a three-story building near the Great Bazaar's center. Accepts virtually every currency in this module. Hours: always (Sigil doesn't sleep).

| Service | Fee | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Standard currency exchange (any hard currency → jinx) | 2–3% | The base rate |
| Standard currency exchange (Prime coinage → jinx) | 3–5% | Slightly higher for less-common currencies |
| Rare currency exchange (obscure Prime; extinct realms) | 10% + authentication fee | The only place in Sigil that routinely handles these |
| Metaphysical currency exchange (belief tokens, etc.) | 5–15% | Handled through specialized officers |
| Soul shells (refused at main counter) | N/A | Refer to Hive Ward operators |
| Direct planar transfer | 2% + time delay | Funds transferred to Consortium offices on any of 12 Outer Planes |
| Large-transaction consulting | 50 gp/hour | For transactions exceeding 100,000 jinx |
| Currency insurance | 0.5–2% of insured value | Against loss in transit |
| Emergency authentication | 150 gp flat | 3× standard fee; 2-hour turnaround |

**The Authentication Bureau:** On the second floor, the Authentication Bureau handles complex cases. Senior authenticators include:
- **Harrick Brightsilver** (dwarf, Appraise +35): Chief authenticator; 40 years' experience; has personally examined every major currency in the multiverse at least once
- **The Twins** (identical tiefling sisters): Specialists in metaphysical currency authentication; handle Soul Shell grading, Belief Token verification, and Memory Pearl content assessment
- **Master Tallowbone** (ancient gnome, Appraise +30): Historical specialist; if a PC brings in a coin from a civilization that fell three thousand years ago, Tallowbone can identify it

### II.9.2 — SHEMESHKA'S CURRENCY BAZAAR (Lady's Ward, Sigil)

The arcanaloth Shemeshka the Marauder operates one of Sigil's most important non-Consortium money-changing operations. A tall, narrow building — nondescript outside, luxuriously appointed within. Not a bank: a currency speculator, information broker, and influence peddler who also exchanges currency.

| Service | Fee | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Standard currency exchange | 4% | Slightly higher than Consortium, but private |
| "Discreet" exchange (no documentation) | 8% | The Consortium would call it "illegal" |
| Exchange of "complicated" wealth | 10–25% | Stolen goods, laundered soul-shells, illicit artifacts — she accepts it all |
| Information trading as part of exchange | Varies | A PC may discover their conversion fee is partly paid in information |
| Letters of credit payable in "unusual" places | 5–15% | Shemeshka has agents on more planes than the Consortium admits to |

**A PC doing business with Shemeshka should expect:**
1. Their transaction to be remembered forever
2. The possibility that Shemeshka will, someday, call in the implicit favor this represents
3. Absolute professional discretion in the interim

---

### II.9.3 — HIVE WARD BLACK MARKETEERS (Sigil)

The Hive Ward hosts the city's unregulated currency exchange markets — a shifting network of operators. Notable:

- **"One-Eye" Pell** (human woman, patch): Operates from a cellar beneath a derelict chapel. Specializes in Lower Plane currencies. Fair in rough circumstances.
- **The Kenku Brothers (Sekta and Kyra):** Two kenku; specialize in transitive plane and exotic location currencies. Speak in repeated overheard phrases — negotiations are confusing but their work is solid.
- **Madame Ochre** (tiefling, 80-year-old veteran): Matriarch of Hive Ward money-changing. Specializes in soul shells — the Consortium won't touch them, but she has built a reputation for fair grading.
- **The Alley of Coins:** A specific alley where a dozen small money-changers operate from stalls and wagons. Rapid, chaotic, dangerous, but the rates are aggressive.

| Service | Fee | Risk Level |
|---|---|---|
| Standard exchange | 5–10% | Moderate |
| Unusual currency | 10–20% | Moderate-High |
| Soul shell exchange | 15–30% | High (legally and morally fraught) |
| Fenced goods conversion | 20–40% | Very High |
| Currency laundering | 30–50% | Extreme |

### II.9.4 — THE MODRON CURRENCY KIOSK (Lower Ward, Sigil)

Near the portal to Mechanus, the modrons maintain a currency exchange kiosk staffed by a single monodrone whose sole function is currency exchange. A small, perfectly square building of black iron with one window and no decoration.

**Properties:**
- Absolute reliability: the monodrone never makes a mistake
- No negotiation whatsoever: cannot be bargained with, flattered, threatened, or confused
- Perfect mathematical accuracy to 0.0001 gp
- Fixed fees: 2% on any exchange, no exceptions
- Limited currencies: accepts jinx, Celestian Trade Bars, Styx-Iron, Gear-Marks, and standard gp. Refuses Chaos Shards, soul shells, and all metaphysical currencies (on philosophical grounds)
- Queue-based service: processes transactions in exact order received

### II.9.6 — PLANE-SPECIFIC MONEY-CHANGING

**City of Brass (Plane of Fire):** The Marketplace of Eternal Flame is staffed by efreet who handle currency exchange for visitors. Rates: 10%. High fee reflects the efreeti view that doing business with outsiders is a favor to the outsider.

**Great Dismal Delve (Plane of Earth):** The Silent Exchange provides currency services for non-dao visitors. Rates 8–12%; services are reliable.

**Azure-Below-Azure (Plane of Water):** The House of Tides is the most sophisticated banking institution on the Plane of Water, offering full services at 5% exchange rates plus 1% annual storage.

**Aaqa (Plane of Air):** The Skyport Consortium provides pleasant and efficient services at 3% exchange rates.

**Upper Planes (Celestia, Bytopia, Arcadia):** Each has institutional banking — the Archon Archives on Celestia, the Gnomish Commercial Cooperatives on Bytopia, the Civic Offices on Arcadia. All are reliable, well-regulated, and welcoming of good-aligned customers.

**Lower Planes:** The Iron Banks of Dis on Baator provide sophisticated banking for those willing to sign the contracts. The Gehenna Mercenary Exchange handles yugoloth-adjacent commerce. On the Grey Waste, night hag operations dominate. On Carceri, the Abyss, and Pandemonium, there is no reliable money-changing — bring what you need.

**Shadowfell:** Gloomwrought hosts the **Dim Vault** — a large fetchling-run operation. Rates: 5% exchange; limited banking for Shadow-Silk and Nightshilling storage. Reliable within the Shadowfell; letters of credit not valid outside.

**Feywild:** Fey markets exist, but Fey Bargains are the real currency. Formal money-changing is offered only at the neutral markets that drift between Summer and Winter Courts. Rates: 10% plus whatever additional "consideration" the fey merchant decides is appropriate. The "friction" of fey commerce is often not the monetary cost but the intangible cost (lost memory, borrowed time, etc.).

**Domains of Dread:** Most domains have local money-changers but few handle cross-plane commerce. Outside wealth is often simply refused or heavily discounted. The Domains' economic sealing is part of their metaphysical nature.

**Athas:** No formal banking; no exchange infrastructure beyond peer-to-peer barter.

### II.9.8 — EMERGENCY CURRENCY CONVERSION

When a PC is stranded without accessible money-changing infrastructure:

1. **Barter directly:** Trade goods or services for what you need. See Module V for labor rates.
2. **Sell minor magical items to local spellcasters:** A potion or scroll is universally valued.
3. **Gem-based payment:** Gems are near-universally accepted, though you'll lose 15–30% on conversion.
4. **Pay in favors:** "I'll repay you when I can." Requires Charisma checks and creates future obligations.
5. **Use a Tier 6+ NPC's influence:** Call in a favor with a wealthy patron or factional ally.
6. **Contact the Revolutionary League:** They maintain emergency aid networks for certain categories of traveler, though their help always comes with expected future reciprocity.

**GM Advice:** Currency emergencies are valuable story beats. A PC suddenly unable to pay for something they need creates drama that predictable wealth management cannot. Use it — but don't abuse it.

## II.10 — QUICK-REFERENCE CARD

*Print this page and keep it behind the GM screen.*

**Hard Currencies:**
- 1 Jinx = 1 gp (universal)
- 1 Styx-Iron Bar = 50 gp (Lower Planes + Sigil)
- 1 Celestian Trade Bar = 100 gp (Upper Planes + Sigil)
- Quintessence: 10 gp/dram, decays 5–15%/day off-Astral

**Prime World Conversion Shortcuts:**
- Oerth, Toril: 1:1 with gp
- Krynn: 1 gp = 1 steel piece (Krynnish gold = 0.5 gp)
- Athas: 1 gp = 100 ceramic pieces; metal is precious

**Friction Rule of Thumb:**
- Same zone → 0–5% friction
- Adjacent zones → 5–10% friction
- Opposed zones → 10–20% friction
- "Wrong currency for this plane" → 15–25% friction
- Domains of Dread out → 20–30% (if at all)

**Elemental Plane Shortcuts:**
- Air: Zephyr Mark = 1 gp (5% friction off-plane)
- Earth: Earth-Coin = 0.9 gp (10% friction off-plane)
- Fire: Ember-Coin = 0.9 gp (10% friction off-plane)
- Water: Tide-Mark = 0.95 gp (5% friction off-plane)
- Para/Quasi-elemental: Barter dominates; hard currency at 10–15% friction

**Outer Plane Currency Shortcuts:**
- Good Planes: Celestian Trade Bars preferred
- Evil Planes: Styx-Iron preferred
- Mechanus: Gear-Marks (perfect 1:1 with jinx)
- Limbo: Chaos Shards (roll for value)
- Outlands: Everything works

**Wealth Tier Shortcut:**
- Tier 1–3: Normal adventuring party, levels 1–5
- Tier 4–6: Mid-campaign, levels 5–12
- Tier 7–8: Endgame, levels 12–20 (WBL cap = Tier 8)
- Tier 9+: Narrative ascension only (kingdom, demiplane, faction leadership)

**Three Questions When a PC Wants to Buy Something:**
1. What currency do they have? (If jinx in Sigil → no friction, done.)
2. What currency does the seller want? (Match → done. Mismatch → apply friction.)
3. What plane? (Apply PPM from Module III.)

**If in Doubt:**
- In Sigil: everything works, small friction, done.
- Outside Sigil: apply friction, check PPM, make it a story beat.
- On a hostile plane with wrong currency: make it an adventure.
- Tier 9+ PC: the campaign is different now. Adventures are political and institutional.
