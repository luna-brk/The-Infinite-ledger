Preamble Design Philosophy ▾

This system rests on three pillars: Coherence, PF1e Compatibility, and Player Fascination.

Coherence — Every price, exchange rate, and trade good must survive the question "but why can't I just buy that on the Plane of Fire?" The answer is always rooted in scarcity, planar physics, faction politics, or metaphysical law.

PF1e Compatibility — Wealth-By-Level (WBL) is the structural spine. The system never violates it; it contextualizes it. A 10th-level character has ~62,000 gp of net worth whether they earned it in Greyhawk gold crowns or Sigil jinx. The economy provides flavor, friction, and player agency around that number — it does not inflate or deflate it.

Player Fascination — The economy is a toy, not a tax. Players should want to arbitrage soul shells between the Grey Waste and Bytopia. They should argue about whether to pay the Merkhant Consortium in belief-tokens or raw quintessence. The complexity is opt-in: a player who just wants to buy a sword can buy a sword. A player who wants to become a planar trade magnate has a system that rewards it.

⚖ The Cardinal Rule

The Infinite Ledger enriches the game without inflating it. No mechanic here allows a PC to exceed or fall below expected Wealth-By-Level by more than 20% for longer than one level. The economy provides texture — flavor, agency, narrative weight — around the WBL number, but it does not alter the number itself.

§ 1.1 Key Definitions ▾

The following terms are used consistently throughout all modules. Understanding them is prerequisite to using the rest of the Ledger.

Term Definition
Standard Gold Piece (gp) The PF1e abstract unit of value. All prices in this system can always be resolved to gp for mechanical purposes. Flavor currencies exist on top of this abstraction, not in place of it.
Local Scrip Any currency that only holds value in a specific location or plane. Oerthian gold lions, Baatorian soul-credits, Mechanus gear-marks.
Hard Currency Currency accepted across multiple planes. The four hard currencies are: Sigilian Jinx, Minted Styx-Iron, Celestian Trade Bars, and Raw Quintessence.
Belief Value (BV) A meta-economic unit representing the power of collective conviction. Some Outer Plane economies literally run on faith. This is the most volatile and dangerous currency in the system.
Exchange Friction The percentage lost when converting between currencies. Represents money-changers, planar instability, trust deficits, and metaphysical incompatibility. Ranges from 0% (jinx-to-jinx in Sigil) to 40%+ (Baatorian soul-credits in Elysium).
Planar Price Modifier (PPM) A multiplier applied to the base PF1e price of goods depending on where you're buying them and what plane the good originates from.
Wealth-By-Level (WBL) PF1e's expected character wealth at each level. The sacred number. This system bends around it, never breaks it. Reference table in Module VIII.
Availability Factor (AF) How easily a given item can be found in a given market. AF ×1.0 = normally stocked. AF ×3.0 = requires a quest or specialist. Covered in detail in Module III.
⚠ On the gp Abstraction

Never forget that gp is a convenience abstraction, not a physical object in Sigil. A planewalker dealing in Sigilian Jinx, Mechanus Gear-Marks, and Bytopian labor-notes is doing exactly the same thing as a level 10 PC with 62,000 gp worth of gear — the numbers just look different. The Ledger provides conversion tables so that every exotic transaction can always resolve to a gp equivalent if the GM needs one.

§ 1.2 The Five Economic Zones ▾

All planar commerce falls into one of five zones, each with its own rules of trade. The zones are ordered by distance from the Prime Material, corresponding roughly to when a campaign encounters them.

Zone 1
The Prime Material
CurrencyMetal coinage (gp/sp/cp/pp standard)
EconomyClassical mercantile. Supply and demand. Mundane.
Key FeatureBaseline. All PF1e prices are "as printed" here.
ScarcityGeography, politics, seasonal availability.
PC ExperienceLevels 1–3. Familiar and grounded. The weirdness is foreshadowed.
Zone 2
The Transitive Planes
CurrencyHard currencies only; local scrip is worthless. Barter dominates.
EconomyFrontier/black-market. No formal institutions.
Key FeatureEverything is portable. Prices are volatile.
ScarcityNothing is manufactured here. Everything is imported or scavenged.
PC ExperienceFirst taste of "your gold doesn't work here." Forces inventory thinking.
Zone 3
The Inner / Elemental Planes
CurrencyElemental commodities (raw flame, primal water, compressed earth, bound air).
EconomyResource extraction. Think mining towns and boom economies.
Key FeatureInfinite raw materials, but refining and transport are expensive.
ScarcityEnergy, transport, and the hostility of the environment itself.
PC ExperienceThe realization that "infinite resources" doesn't mean "infinite wealth."
Zone 4
The Outer Planes
CurrencyWildly variable — barter to belief-tokens to soul-based economies.
EconomyIdeological. The economy reflects the philosophy of the plane.
Key FeatureOn Mechanus every price is fixed. On Limbo prices are whatever you can convince reality they are. On the Lower Planes, souls are legal tender.
ScarcityAlignment, belief, planar law, the will of the powers.
PC ExperienceWhere the economy becomes truly alien. A paladin must grapple with Carceri's only currency: suffering.
Zone 5 — The Hub
Sigil, the City of Doors
CurrencyThe Sigilian Jinx (hard currency). Everything else accepted at exchange friction.
EconomyFree port. The ultimate bazaar. Every economy in the multiverse collides here.
Key FeatureSigil is where you convert Baatorian soul-credits into Bytopian labor-notes. The exchange rates are the adventure hooks.
PC ExperienceLevels 3–20. Home base. The players learn to navigate Sigil's economy as a core competency.
§ 1.3 The Planar Arbitrage Problem ▾

This is the elephant in the room. If iron is worthless on the Plane of Earth and priceless on the Plane of Fire, why can't a player just haul iron between planes and become infinitely rich?

⚠ The Core Question

Every obvious arbitrage opportunity has already been noticed. The PC is not the first person to see this price differential. Established traders with institutional advantages, political connections, and established routes have been there for decades. Module VIII provides the complete Anti-Exploit Toolkit for GMs.

The system addresses this through five interlocking safeguards:

  • Safeguard I
    Transport Costs. Moving material between planes is expensive. Portal tolls, planar conduit fees, hired protection, carrying capacity, and the sheer danger of the journey eat into margins. Module IV contains a full transport cost engine.
  • Safeguard II
    Market Saturation. Planar markets are not infinitely liquid. If a player floods the City of Brass with cheap iron, the efreet respond — violently, economically, or both. Saturation mechanics are in Module III.
  • Safeguard III
    Faction Interference. The Merkhant Consortium and the Fated don't appreciate freelancers undercutting their trade routes. The Harmonium doesn't allow unlicensed interplanar commerce. Faction responses scale with the size of the operation. Module VI covers this.
  • Safeguard IV
    The WBL Governor. The GM always has final say. Module VIII provides explicit guidance for adjusting treasure awards, imposing costs, and creating narrative consequences to keep PC wealth on track. This isn't a penalty — it's a feature. The world responds to economic disruption the same way it responds to a dragon attack: with consequences.
  • Safeguard V
    Opportunity Cost. Time spent arbitraging is time not spent adventuring. The system makes trade profitable enough to be interesting but never more lucrative per hour than dungeon-crawling. This is calibrated in Module VIII.
GM Note — The Substitution Principle

When a PC earns wealth through the planar economy (trade, services, faction benefits), that wealth substitutes for treasure the GM would otherwise have awarded. The PC earns differently, not additionally. The reward is agency — the player chose how to earn their wealth. The total remains WBL-compliant.

§ 1.4 Currency System Overview (Module II Preview) ▾

The complete currency system is documented in Module II — Currencies & Mediums of Exchange. The tables below provide a working preview sufficient for early-campaign play.

Hard Currencies — Accepted Across Multiple Planes

Currency Origin Base Value Accepted Where Notes
Sigilian Jinx Sigil 1 jinx = 1 gp Everywhere that trades with Sigil The de facto reserve currency of the multiverse. Minted by the Merkhant Consortium under dabus oversight. Cannot be counterfeited (the Lady's mark).
Minted Styx-Iron Lower Planes 1 bar = 50 gp Lower Planes, Sigil, Outlands Iron from the River Styx, forged in Gehenna. Carries a faint aura of forgetfulness. Accepted because it's useful for binding fiends.
Celestian Trade Bar Upper Planes 1 bar = 100 gp Upper Planes, Sigil, Outlands, some Prime worlds Sanctified silver stamped with the seal of a celestial concordance. Self-authenticating (glows faintly in the presence of evil).
Raw Quintessence Astral Plane Variable — see Module II Everywhere, but perishable The raw stuff of creation. Decays outside the Astral. Functions as a universal solvent of value but degrades ~10% per day on other planes unless contained.

Exotic / Metaphysical Currencies — Plane-Specific

Currency Plane(s) Nature Convertibility
Soul Shells Lower Planes Harvested petitioner essences Converts to gp at appalling rates. Morally catastrophic. Illegal in Sigil.
Belief Tokens Outer Planes (various) Crystallized faith Value depends on the deity/philosophy and the buyer's alignment.
Elemental Motes Inner Planes Condensed elemental matter Valuable as crafting materials. Convert to gp via artisan markets.
Modron Gear-Marks Mechanus Standardized units of labor Perfectly stable. Perfectly boring. 1 gear-mark = 1 gp, always, forever.
Chaos Shards Limbo Frozen chunks of possibility Value fluctuates wildly. Roll 1d6 × base value at each transaction.
Memory Pearls Various (esp. Astral) Stored experiences / knowledge Value depends entirely on the memory contained. Highly prized by the Society of Sensation.
