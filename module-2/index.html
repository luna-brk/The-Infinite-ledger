Currencies & Mediums of Exchange

Coinage, Barter-Tokens, and Metaphysical Tender
◆ Active Reference

This module defines the sanctioned mediums through which planar commerce is conducted, the conversion rates observed by the Consortium, and the "Friction" costs of ideology.

MODULE OVERVIEW (Overview) ▾

This module catalogs every currency, medium of exchange, and store of value in the multiverse — from the copper pip a Cager presses into a pie-seller's palm, to the mythic soul-treasuries controlled by archdevils and demon princes. It is organized from the most universal and stable currencies down to the most exotic, dangerous, and reality-bending. Every entry includes: physical description, denomination structure, who issues it, where it's accepted, how to authenticate it, how to convert it, and what happens when things go wrong.

This module covers, without omission:

  • The four Hard Currencies that form the backbone of interplanar trade
  • Four major Prime Material worlds (Oerth/Greyhawk, Toril/Forgotten Realms, Athas/Dark Sun, Krynn/Dragonlance) with full regional detail and the Underdark as a sub-zone
  • Additional Prime worlds for reference (Eberron, Mystara, Birthright, Spelljammer networks)
  • The Transitive Planes in full: the Astral, the Ethereal, the Shadow Plane, and the theorized Ordial
  • The Feywild and its Courts in detail, with fey-specific currency mechanics
  • The Shadowfell and its major markets in detail
  • The Domains of Dread as a sealed demiplane-cluster with unique economic laws
  • All four Classical Elemental Planes (Air, Earth, Fire, Water) with archomental politics
  • All four Para-Elemental Planes (Ice, Magma, Ooze, Smoke)
  • All eight Quasi-Elemental Planes (Lightning, Minerals, Radiance, Steam — Positive; Ash, Dust, Salt, Vacuum — Negative)
  • The Positive Energy Plane and the Negative Energy Plane
  • All sixteen Outer Planes (the Great Ring in full) plus the Outlands and Sigil
  • A complete Wealth Spectrum from beggary to cosmic economic dominion — Tiers 1 through 12

How to use this module: The Hard Currencies in II.1 are the spine — learn these first. Regional and planar currencies (II.2 through II.5) are reference material for when the party arrives somewhere specific. Metaphysical currencies (II.6) come into play around levels 5–7. Conversion tables (II.7) are your table-side reference. The Wealth Spectrum (II.8) is what "rich" means at every tier, including the ultra-wealthy who field armies, run intelligence networks, and reshape the economic destiny of entire planes.

PF1e WBL Anchor: All prices and wealth values in this module are calibrated to PF1e Wealth by Level. The economy contextualizes WBL; it does not violate it. A 10th-level character has approximately 62,000 gp net worth in whatever form that wealth takes. A 20th-level character has approximately 760,000 gp. The ultra-wealthy (Tiers 9–12) are narrative constructs — kings, faction leaders, demon lords — whose wealth exists as a story resource, not as a PC-accessible windfall.

HARD CURRENCIES (Overview) ▾

Hard currencies are accepted across multiple planes and function as the load-bearing pillars of interplanar commerce. There are four, each backed by different forms of trust. Learn these four currencies before anything else in this module.

§ 2.1.1 THE SIGILIAN JINX ▾

The Reserve Currency of the Multiverse

Physical Description

A jinx is a small, irregularly shaped coin — no two are exactly alike. Each is stamped from an alloy called dusksteel, a blue-grey metal smelted exclusively in Sigil from ores gathered across a dozen planes. The obverse bears the symbol of the Merkhant Consortium (an open hand crossed by a key). The reverse bears a shifting, maze-like pattern that is subtly different on every coin — this is the Lady's Mark, and it is what makes counterfeiting impossible. The Mark doesn't shimmer or glow; it simply is wrong when replicated. Forgeries feel slippery, unsettling. Authentic jinx feel like certainty.

A jinx is slightly smaller than a standard gold piece and weighs roughly the same (50 to the pound, PF1e standard coin weight).

On higher denominations: The Grand Bar and above are rarely seen in casual circulation. A Trade Bar is the largest coin most Sigilites handle in a year. Vault Ingots live almost exclusively in Consortium vaults; their transactions are conducted through paperwork rather than physical movement. The Foundry Monolith is the institutional reserve unit of the wealthiest merchant princes, senior faction leaders, and powerful extraplanar lords. The Lady's Weight is more rumor than reality — a denomination that exists in the Consortium's ledger system but whose physical form is perhaps a formality.

Issuance & Minting

The Merkhant Consortium holds the exclusive right to mint jinx in Sigil. Minting occurs in the Foundry Quarter, under direct observation by dabus — who apply the Lady's Mark to each coin through a process no mortal has ever successfully observed or replicated. The Consortium pays the Lady's fee for this service, though what that fee consists of is one of the great mysteries of Sigil's economy. The most popular theory: a percentage of all transactions conducted in jinx flows to the Lady as information. She doesn't want gold — she wants knowledge of what moves through her city.

New jinx enter circulation through Consortium exchange windows (where foreign currency is converted) and through Consortium lending operations. The total supply of jinx is not public knowledge. The Consortium claims it is carefully managed; the Fated claim it is inflated for Consortium profit; the Fraternity of Order has been trying to audit the Consortium for three centuries and has been politely stonewalled every time.

Acceptance

  • Everywhere in Sigil — universal, no exceptions
  • Throughout the Outlands — as readily as local coinage, often preferred
  • On most Outer Planes — with varying degrees of enthusiasm (see plane-specific notes)
  • On the Inner/Elemental Planes — accepted at major trade posts and crossroads; less so in deep interiors
  • On the Transitive Planes — accepted at githyanki outposts, astral trading posts, and ethereal waypoints
  • On the Shadowfell — accepted in Gloomwrought and at established markets; less elsewhere
  • In the Feywild — accepted cautiously; fey prefer their own currencies or Bargains
  • In the Domains of Dread — accepted only in cosmopolitan domains; sealed domains refuse or don't recognize them
  • On the Prime Material — accepted by planar-aware merchants only; to ordinary Primes, a jinx is a strange foreign coin worth approximately 0.5 gp as metal

Authentication

Method DC Notes
Casual inspection (familiar handler) Automatic Anyone who's handled jinx regularly recognizes them by feel
Visual inspection (unfamiliar handler) Appraise DC 10 The maze pattern is distinctive
Detecting a forgery Appraise DC 15 Forgeries feel subtly wrong — a mild unease
Detecting a high-quality forgery Appraise DC 22 Rare. The maze pattern is almost right but causes faint nausea on close study
Magical authentication Detect Magic Reveals faint abjuration (the Lady's Mark). Forgeries lack this aura entirely.

Counterfeiting

It has been tried. It always fails. The Lady's Mark is not a spell — it is an expression of the Lady's will, and no known magic replicates it. The most famous attempt was by a cabal of Guvners who spent forty years analyzing the Mark's pattern mathematically. They produced a coin that passed visual inspection and even radiated faint abjuration. The Lady mazed all seven of them on the day they tried to spend it. Their coins are displayed in the Consortium's headquarters as a warning.

Mechanical Ruling: Counterfeiting jinx is impossible within the scope of this system. If a player insists on attempting it, the DC is ∞. If they find some extraordinary means (a wish, divine intervention), the Lady responds personally. This is a hard boundary.

Letters of Credit and Banking Instruments

For transactions above 100 jinx, the Merkhant Consortium issues letters of credit — folded sheets of vellum bearing the Consortium's seal and the Lady's Mark. These function as bank drafts, redeemable at any Consortium exchange window in Sigil or at major offices on the Outlands.

Letters of credit are not bearer instruments. They are keyed to a specific individual via magical imprint (a drop of blood, a lock of hair, or a spoken true name — customer's choice). Only the designated recipient can cash them, though formal transfer is possible at a Consortium office for a 3% transfer fee.

For the ultra-wealthy (balances exceeding 1,000,000 jinx), the Consortium offers Private Banking:

  • Custom investment portfolios (shares in Consortium ventures, trade syndicate positions, demiplane bonds)
  • Personal letter-of-credit courier service (a planar messenger maintains a route to wherever you are)
  • Access to the Consortium's most discreet currency-exchange services
  • Informal voice in Consortium policy matters
  • Priority access to the Great Bazaar's restricted supply tiers
  • Sovereign wealth management (for clients managing the finances of entire cities, kingdoms, or planar territories)
§ 2.1.2 MINTED STYX-IRON ▾

The Currency of the Damned

Physical Description

Styx-Iron is exactly what it sounds like: iron dredged from the River Styx and forged in the volcanic workshops of Gehenna. It is black with an oily, iridescent sheen — like a raven's wing dipped in petroleum. It is cold to the touch regardless of ambient temperature and carries a faint psychic residue of the memories the Styx has dissolved. Handling Styx-Iron for extended periods (more than an hour of continuous skin contact) causes mild disorientation and short-term memory fuzziness — nothing mechanically significant for a few hours, but unsettling.

Styx-Iron is minted into flat, rectangular bars of standardized weight. No imagery is stamped — the Lower Planes do not care for symbolism on their currency. Value is determined purely by weight and purity.

Denominations

Denomination Weight Value (gp) Notes
Styx Sliver 1 oz 5 gp Small, palm-sized bar. Pocket currency.
Styx Bar (standard) 10 oz 50 gp Standard trade unit. About the size of a deck of cards.
Styx Ingot 50 oz 250 gp Brick-sized. Serious transactions only.
Styx Block 200 oz 1,000 gp Requires two hands to carry. Used between fiendish organizations.
Styx Slab 1,000 oz (62.5 lb) 5,000 gp Institutional quantities. Moved on carts.
Styx Plinth 10,000 oz (625 lb) 50,000 gp Stored in fortified vaults. Used between Hell's pit lords and major yugoloth companies.
Styx Monolith 100,000 oz (6,250 lb) 500,000 gp In practice, held by archdevils and the Oinoloth. Moving one requires a significant operation.
Styx Colossal 1,000,000 oz (62,500 lb) 5,000,000 gp Theoretical denomination. Represents the treasury reserve of an entire layer of Hell. Transacted only through paperwork; the physical object is kept in Nessus or Gehenna's deepest vault.

Why It's Valuable

  1. Binding Efficacy. Chains, shackles, and circles made of Styx-Iron impose a –2 penalty on escape attempts (both mundane and magical) by fiends. Critical security material in the Lower Planes, where betrayal is a way of life.
  2. Memory Erosion. Weapons forged from Styx-Iron carry a trace of the Styx's memory-devouring properties. A creature struck must make a Will save (DC 12) or lose one round's worth of short-term memory — they forget what happened in the round they were struck.
  3. Planar Stability. Styx-Iron does not rust, corrode, or degrade on any Lower Plane. It is the most physically durable mundane metal in existence when kept in those environments.

Issuance & Sourcing

Styx-Iron is not "issued" by any central authority. It is harvested — dragged from the banks and shallows of the River Styx by teams of enslaved petitioners, minor fiends, or desperate mortals. The largest harvesting operations are run by the yugoloths of Gehenna, who control the most productive stretches of river and operate smelting foundries that purify raw Styx-Iron into currency-grade bars. The night hags of the Grey Waste are the second-largest producers and the primary distributors — they carry Styx-Iron across the Lower Planes as part of their soul-trading operations.

Acceptance

  • Lower Planes — Universal. The default currency across Baator, Gehenna, the Grey Waste, Carceri, the Abyss, and Pandemonium.
  • Sigil — Accepted by the Merkhant Consortium at standard exchange rates. Many Sigil merchants accept it directly with a 5–10% premium for the inconvenience.
  • Outlands — Accepted at gate-towns near Lower Plane portals (Ribcage, Torch, Hopeless, Plague-Mort, Curst, Automata's black market). Not accepted in Upper Plane-aligned gate-towns.
  • Domains of Dread — Accepted in many domains, particularly those with fiendish connections.
  • Upper Planes — Not accepted. Styx-Iron radiates faint evil; celestial merchants refuse to touch it on principle.
  • Prime Material — Functionally unknown. If identified (Knowledge [planes] DC 18), it can be sold to planar-aware buyers at base gp value.
  • Shadowfell — Accepted in darker markets; Gloomwrought takes it at a 10% discount.
§ 2.1.3 CELESTIAN TRADE BARS ▾

The Currency of the Righteous

Physical Description

Celestian Trade Bars are slim rectangles of sanctified silver, approximately five inches long, one inch wide, and a quarter-inch thick. Each bears the seal of the Celestian Trade Concordance — a handshake beneath a radiant sun — impressed into both faces. The silver has a warm, faintly luminous quality; it doesn't glow visibly in daylight, but in dim light or darkness, a Celestian Trade Bar emits light equivalent to a candle in a 5-foot radius.

The bars are warm to the touch for good-aligned creatures, neutral-temperature for neutral creatures, and uncomfortably cold for evil-aligned creatures. This property is the bar's primary authentication mechanism.

Denominations

Denomination Value (gp) Physical Form Notes
Celestian Sliver 10 gp Thin strip, 2 inches long Everyday transactions on Upper Planes
Celestian Trade Bar (standard) 100 gp Full bar, 5 inches Standard unit
Celestian Grand Bar 500 gp Larger bar, 8 inches, thicker Major transactions
Celestian Covenant Seal 1,000 gp Circular medallion, palm-sized Issued only for specific large contracts
Celestian Sanctum Plate 10,000 gp Silver disc, 1 foot diameter Used for major temple endowments and institutional transfers
Celestian Throne Bar 100,000 gp Large plate-like ingot, 5 lb Treaty settlements between realms; planar-scale transfers
Celestian Crown Ingot 1,000,000 gp Heavy bar, 25 lb, consecrated Reserved for the most senior Upper Plane institutional transactions; each is individually blessed by an archon of the highest rank

Issuance

Celestian Trade Bars are issued by the Celestian Trade Concordance, a cooperative body representing major celestial commercial interests across the Upper Planes. The Concordance includes representatives from Mount Celestia (archon bankers), Bytopia (gnomish crafters and traders), Elysium (agricultural exporters), and the Beastlands (natural resource managers). Arborea and Ysgard participate intermittently.

Acceptance

  • Upper Planes — Universal. The standard currency of the Upper Planes.
  • Sigil — Accepted by the Merkhant Consortium at standard rates.
  • Outlands — Accepted near Upper Plane gate-towns; less so near Lower Plane gates.
  • Prime Material — Accepted by temple-affiliated merchants, particularly in communities with strong celestial ties.
  • Lower Planes — Refused by evil entities (alignment revulsion). Neutral evil merchants may accept at 20% discount for the inconvenience of handling it.

Authentication

The alignment-temperature response is the key authentication mechanism:

Creature Alignment Bar feels... DC to fake this property
Good Warm, comforting Cannot be faked; property is magical
Neutral Room temperature —
Evil Cold, uncomfortable —

Additional authentication: Detect Good reveals a strong aura. Forgeries made of ordinary silver lack the aura entirely and feel neutral regardless of handler alignment.

§ 2.1.4 QUINTESSENCE ▾

The Currency of the Astral

Physical Description

Quintessence is the solidified residue of thought and time that drifts through the Astral Plane — wisps of silver-grey matter that have condensed from the psychic medium of the plane. When harvested and refined, it becomes a thick, cloudy liquid that moves like mercury but glows faintly with pearlescent light. It is stored in sealed vials of crystal or specially treated glass. Contact with most surfaces causes it to slowly dissolve, which is why storage quality matters enormously.

Denominations and Value

Form Value Notes
Dram (smallest vial) 10 gp The base unit. About 1/8th teaspoon.
Standard Vial 40 gp Standard trade unit; 4 drams
Flask 200 gp 20 drams; a significant store of value
Phial (sealed crystal) 500 gp Premium storage; decay rate significantly reduced
Distilled Quintessence (flask) 1,000 gp Refined for purity; slower decay; preferred by high-end buyers
Philosopher's Vial (great crystal) 10,000 gp Extraordinary purity; used as component for the highest magical workings
Astral Sovereign (sealed monument) 100,000 gp A vessel the size of an urn containing near-perfectly preserved quintessence of extraordinary purity. One of the rarest tradeable commodities in existence; held only by githyanki Queen's treasury and a few astral merchant princes.

The Decay Problem

Quintessence is uniquely perishable: it evaporates at a rate determined by storage quality and plane:

Condition Daily Decay Rate
On the Astral Plane, in standard vessel 0% (stable)
On the Astral Plane, in open air 5% per hour
On the Prime Material, in good vessel 5% per day
On the Prime Material, in poor vessel 15% per day
On any Elemental Plane 10% per day
On any Outer Plane 3% per day
In a Githyanki Stability Matrix (specialized container) 0% regardless of plane

Properties

Quintessence has genuine magical utility beyond its currency value:

  • Time Suspension: A creature or object immersed in quintessence ceases to age, hunger, thirst, bleed, or change until removed. This makes it invaluable for preserving living specimens, sustaining wounded companions, or suspending magical processes indefinitely.
  • Astral Travel Aid: Quintessence amplifies astral projection, reducing the casting time of astral projection by half when consumed as a material component.
  • Magical Catalyst: Used as a spell component for high-level spells involving time, prophecy, memory, or the mind.

Acceptance

  • Astral Plane — Universal among those who know what it is.
  • Sigil — Accepted by the Consortium and specialty magical merchants.
  • Githyanki markets — Premium currency; they produce and store the most.
  • Outer Planes — Accepted by planar-aware traders; most petitioners don't know what it is.
  • Prime Material — Known only to specialists. If identified, valuable; if not, a mysterious glowing liquid.
§ 2.1.5 THE ORDIAL RESONANCE FRAGMENT ▾

Currency of the Dead Between Planes (Rare; Theoretical)

The Ordial Plane — the rumored transitive plane connecting the Outer Planes to each other and to the energy planes — is theorized but not definitively mapped. Those who claim to have traversed it describe fragments of crystallized reality that form at the intersections of planar boundaries: Ordial Resonance Fragments, sometimes called Border-Stones by those who deal in them.

These fragments are not confirmed to be a stable currency — their existence and reliability vary by who you ask. What is known:

Item Value Notes
Ordial Resonance Fragment (minor) 500–2,000 gp A small crystalline shard; used as a planar-travel component
Ordial Resonance Fragment (major) 5,000–20,000 gp Larger, more coherent; used in planar cartography and transit magic
Ordial Meridian (the largest recorded) 100,000+ gp A fragment the size of a fist; allegedly shows the relative positions of nearby planes when held

GM Note: Whether the Ordial truly exists as a navigable plane is a campaign-level mystery. Ordial Fragments as currency represent the bleeding edge of extraplanar commerce — available only to characters deeply embedded in planar politics and exploration.

PRIME MATERIAL CURRENCIES (Overview) ▾

The Prime Material Plane contains thousands of worlds, each with its own economic history. This section covers four major worlds in depth, followed by secondary world references and the Underdark as a sub-zone.

General rule for Prime world currencies: In their home nations, they function at face value. On other Prime worlds, they are typically worth their metal value (the gp value of the coin's raw metal content). To planar traders in Sigil or the Outlands, many Prime coins are simply "gold" or "silver" by weight.

  • City of Greyhawk: Major merchant houses (House Oeridian, the Merchants' and Traders' Union) offer letters of credit valid within the Flanaess. Temple banking through Zilchus's churches provides formal deposit and interest services.
  • Furyondy: Royal Treasury issues letters of credit for large transactions.

Oerth Trade Goods as Currency

In frontier regions, trade goods substitute for coinage:

  • Furs (beaver, sable): 1–10 gp each depending on quality
  • Spices (pepper, saffron, cinnamon): 5–50 gp per lb
  • Quality iron: 1 gp per lb (lower in dwarven regions)
  • Enchanted items: At standard PF1e prices (consult Module III for regional modifiers)

Planar Interface

Most Oerthians have no knowledge of planar commerce. To a merchant in Greyhawk, a Sigilian jinx is a strange foreign coin and is assessed at roughly 0.5 gp (its estimated metal value). Planar-aware merchants (typically those with Knowledge [planes] ranks or guild affiliations with planar contacts) recognize jinx at full value.

The Transcendent Order's small presence on Oerth (primarily a monastery outside Greyhawk) provides the closest thing Oerth has to a currency exchange for interplanar trade — though they offer it as a public service, not a commercial operation.

Toril Banking Infrastructure

Toril has the most sophisticated Prime Material banking in the multiverse:

  • Deposit accounts with 1–2% annual interest
  • Currency exchange (all standard coins, 2–3% fee)
  • Trade financing (secured loans at 8–12%)
  • Merchant-bonding (a temple-guaranteed guarantee of a merchant's creditworthiness)

Planar Interface

Toril has more planar commerce than any other Prime world. The City of Portals (Sigil) does a meaningful fraction of its business with Toril-origin merchants. Torilians recognize jinx at their full value almost universally in major cities. Conversely, well-minted Waterdhavian gold pieces circulate in Sigil at a 5% discount (recognizable as quality metal, but requiring conversion from an unfamiliar mint).

§ 2.2.3 ATHAS (Dark Sun) ▾

The Dying World

Athas is unlike any other Prime world in this system. It is a world of ecological devastation, where centuries of reckless arcane magic (defiling) have stripped the land of most life, turned forests to desert, and left the water scarce and the metal scarcer. Its economy is built around fundamental scarcity rather than abundance, which inverts nearly every standard economic assumption.

The Core Economic Inversion: On Athas, metal is so rare that ceramic has replaced coin as the primary currency. A genuine iron sword is a major piece of wealth. Mithral and adamantine are essentially mythical — encountered only in the most extraordinary circumstances.

Athas-Specific Wealth Storage

The ultra-wealthy on Athas (templars, sorcerer-king officials, high merchant houses) store wealth in:

  • Metal ingots — the ultimate store of value; one pound of iron is worth a small fortune
  • Slave holdings — slavery is normalized on Athas; a skilled slave may represent 500–5,000 gp in value
  • Water rights — controlling a well or oasis is equivalent to owning gold mines
  • Silk Wyrm weave — a rare textile worth enormous sums
  • Preserved food stores — long-term survival assets in a dying world
  • Psionic knowledge — crystallized techniques, not strictly wealth, but valuable

Planar Interface

For planar travelers, Athas is a treasure trove and a nightmare simultaneously. A mithral dagger that would fetch 300 gp in Sigil can sell for 3,000–5,000 Athasian gp equivalent in Balic or Tyr. Adamantine is nearly worship-worthy. Conversely, bringing Athasian ceramic "coins" to Sigil yields only their clay value — essentially nothing.

The Water Trade: Planar travelers who can reliably bring clean water to Athas (via create water services, portable wells, or water-aspected magic) can command extraordinary prices. A half-hour of create water castings, producing 50 gallons, can feed an entire Athasian village for a week and sell for the equivalent of 200–500 gp in metal and trade goods.

Elemental Priests: Athas's unique relationship to the elemental planes means that elemental priests are plentiful and offer elemental services at lower rates than elsewhere. Water-elemental summoning services are particularly valuable.

Kender Economics (Special Note)

Kender are infamous for "handling" (liberating) items that catch their interest. Kender communities have enormous variety of small objects that individual kender have acquired through "handling." PC opportunity: Visit a kender market. Find extraordinary things. Pay laughable prices. The ethical question of whether those items were stolen from someone else is left to the PC's conscience. THE TRANSITIVE PLANES (Overview) ▾

The Transitive Planes are the spaces between other planes — connective tissue of the cosmology. They are sparsely populated and economically marginal in themselves, but they are the highways along which interplanar commerce moves. Understanding their economies means understanding the costs of moving anything across the multiverse.

§ 2.3.1 THE ASTRAL PLANE ▾

Silver Void Between the Outer Planes

The Astral is an infinite silver sea of solidified thought, populated by drifting god-corpses, githyanki fortresses, planar travelers, and the occasional merchant who has set up a waystation. Currency-wise, the Astral is simple: hard currencies only, no local scrip, and the specialty currency is Quintessence (see II.1.4).

Economic Character:

  • Default currency: Hard currencies (primarily jinx and Quintessence)
  • No native currency beyond Quintessence
  • Barter supplements hard currency at smaller posts
  • Githyanki communities operate their own internal currency (Silver Sword Bonds, below)

§ 2.3.2 THE ETHEREAL PLANE ▾

The Mist Between Material and Inner

The Ethereal is the connective space between the Prime Material worlds and the Inner Planes. It is sparsely populated — mostly phase spiders, ether-dwelling creatures, and the occasional wizard conducting ethereal research. Commerce is minimal but not absent.

Economic Character:

  • No native currency whatsoever
  • Barter dominates at the few waystations
  • Accepted currencies: Hard currencies only
  • Special trade goods: Drift-stone, ghost-silk (from ethereal-adjacent hauntings), materials harvested from ethereal creatures (phase spider silk fetches 50 gp/yard)

Phase Spider Silk: The Ethereal's primary trade good. Phase spiders weave silk as they move between the Material and Ethereal; this silk retains partial phase properties:

  • Phase Spider Silk (1 yard): 50 gp
  • Phase Spider Silk Bolt (10 yards): 450 gp (bulk discount)
  • Woven Phase Cloth (garment): 500–2,000 gp depending on quality; grants +2 circumstance bonus on saves vs. ethereal effects

Ethereal Waystations: Small, ad hoc stopping points maintained by various factions. Notable:

  • The Gossamer Helm: A former sailing ship that became ethereal during a storm and never returned. Now a waystation run by its ghost crew; accepts hard currency and phase spider silk as payment.
  • The Blind Hermit's Post: Run by an aasimar who chose to live on the Ethereal as a form of meditation. He sells basic supplies at cost and asks only for conversation.

Shadowfell Economic Mechanics

Memory-Value: On the Shadowfell, items associated with significant memories command premium prices — a sword that belonged to a famous warrior is worth more here than its mundane equivalent, and that premium is proportional to how significant the memory is. Appraise checks to assess memory-value require Knowledge (history) DC 15–25.

Undead Commerce: Intelligent undead are full economic participants. Vampires are among the wealthiest beings in the Shadowfell — they accumulate wealth over centuries of predation and commerce. Liches typically hold their wealth in magical components.

Grief as Currency: In some Shadowfell markets, particularly those near the Raven Queen's domain, demonstrated grief (for a specific, named dead being) functions as a form of payment for information or services related to the deceased. This is not metaphorical — the local magic can tell genuine grief from performance.

§ 2.3.5 THE DOMAINS OF DREAD ▾

The Demiplane of Terror

The Domains of Dread are not a single plane but a cluster of demiplanes — pocket realities controlled by the Dark Powers, ancient entities of unclear nature and unclear motivation. Each domain is ruled by a Darklord, a being of significant power who has been drawn into the Demiplane and is simultaneously its ruler and its prisoner. The Darklords are trapped; they cannot leave. Many don't know it yet.

Economic Character: The Sealed Economy

The Domains of Dread are economically isolated by design. The Dark Powers seal their creation:

  • Exit is impossible for most beings (including PCs who are not on specific quest lines, at the GM's discretion)
  • External currency either doesn't work or is assessed only at metal value
  • Local economies function only within their domain
  • Cross-domain trade exists but is limited and hazardous

This isolation is not metaphorical. A PC who arrives in the Domains of Dread carrying 10,000 jinx will find that jinx are foreign objects worth their metal (roughly 0.5 gp each) in most domains — or, in the more paranoid domains, evidence of planar origin that attracts dangerous attention.

Entering the Domains with Wealth

PCs who enter the Domains of Dread with significant outside wealth face immediate practical problems:

  1. Recognition: Jinx and Trade Bars mark you as an outsider. In most domains, this is dangerous — outsiders are feared, persecuted, or hunted.

GM Guidance: The Domains of Dread should feel economically claustrophobic. Players used to the free commerce of Sigil should experience the sealed economy as a form of the Domains' horror — not just monsters, but the existential dread of a world that won't let you leave, including your money.

§ 2.3.6 THE FEYWILD (Plane of Faerie) ▾

The Bright Mirror of the Material

The Feywild is the Material Plane's luminous reflection — a realm where every forest is ancient, every meadow is magic, every river has a name and opinions, and the rules of time and reality are suggestions rather than laws. Its economy is correspondingly alien: not corrupt, not broken, just operating on fundamentally different assumptions than mortal economics.

The Core Feywild Economic Principle: In the Feywild, what something means matters as much as what it is. A sword worth 300 gp in Sigil might be worth 3,000 gp in the Feywild if it was forged on a specific festival night — or 30 gp if it's iron (which the fey find uncomfortable) and was made by someone with poor intent. Value is contextual, narrative, and frankly arbitrary by mortal standards.

Fey Market Rules

The floating fey markets that drift between courts operate under specific rules:

  1. Iron is banned. Iron weapons and tools cannot be brought to most fey markets. Attempting to do so will trigger an immediate — and possibly violent — response.
  2. Time is different. A PC who spends "an afternoon" in a fey market may emerge to find a week, a month, or a year has passed. All purchases made should be noted with this risk in mind.
  3. Flattery has economic value. Sincere compliments about a fey merchant's goods can reduce prices by 5–15%. (Insincere flattery achieves the opposite effect — fey can always tell.)
  4. Names have power. Giving your true name to a fey merchant grants them leverage. Don't do it. Use a title or a nickname.
  5. Prices are personal. The same item may cost different amounts for different buyers depending on what the fey merchant wants from each customer.

  • Material Plane: Feywild goods brought to the Material gradually lose their special properties over months to years; enchantments may fade, foods stop being magically nutritious.
  • Shadowfell: Feywild and Shadowfell goods are thematic opposites. Shadowfell merchants will pay premium for Feywild goods (and vice versa) because neither can easily source them domestically.
THE INNER PLANES (Overview) ▾

The Inner Planes are the building blocks of physical reality — vast, hostile, and economically defined by what they produce rather than what they trade. Commerce here is not about culture or ideology (as on the Outer Planes) but about extracting and moving the fundamental materials of existence. The Inner Planes are the multiverse's industrial zones.

General rules for Inner Plane commerce:

  • Hard currencies (especially jinx) are accepted at major trade nodes and by established merchants; barter dominates in the deep interior
  • Elemental creatures are not inherently commercial; many are indifferent to mortal wealth and must be approached with appropriate trade goods
  • The archomentals (the elemental lords, good and evil) control the major trade routes on their respective planes and take tribute from traffic
  • Environmental protection is mandatory and expensive — without it, you'll die before you can spend anything
§ 2.4.1 THE PLANE OF AIR ▾

THE OUTER PLANES (Overview) ▾

The Outer Planes are the realms of belief, ideology, and alignment made manifest. If the Inner Planes are foundations of physics, the Outer Planes are foundations of meaning. Here, economies are not shaped by scarcity or environmental hostility but by the philosophy of the plane itself. Mount Celestia's economy runs on bureaucratic virtue; Baator's on contractual rapacity; Limbo's on chaos literalized.

For the PC who reaches level 5+ and begins plane-hopping seriously, the Outer Planes provide the richest and strangest economic experiences in the multiverse. They are also where the greatest rewards and the greatest risks lie. A fair deal on Mount Celestia might bless your weapons. A fair deal in Baator might cost your soul.

§ 2.5.1 THE SEVEN MOUNTING HEAVENS OF CELESTIA (Lawful Good) ▾

Economic Character: The Celestian economy is defined by three principles: fairness, documentation, and patience. The plane's "exports" are primarily services: healing, blessing, crafting of holy items, spiritual counsel, and arbitration of conflicts.

METAPHYSICAL CURRENCIES (Overview) ▾

These currencies are not material objects — they are crystallized aspects of reality itself. They are the most powerful, most valuable, and most dangerous mediums of exchange in the multiverse. Buying something with a soul shell is not "using a rare coin." It is spending something that was once a person.

PCs encountering metaphysical currencies for the first time — usually around levels 5–7 — should feel the weight of what they're handling. These are not just valuable items. They are ontological objects that raise questions about consciousness, morality, and reality itself.

§ 2.6.1 SOUL SHELLS ▾

The Most Controversial Currency in Existence

What They Are

When a petitioner — the post-mortem spiritual residue of a mortal — is "harvested" (killed, consumed, or drained on the Outer Planes), the remaining essence can be crystallized into a small, translucent shell approximately the size and shape of a robin's egg. This is a soul shell. It contains the distilled spiritual energy of a being that once lived, thought, loved, and feared.

Grading System

Soul shells are graded by the night hag soul-brokers who dominate the trade:

Grade Source Value (gp) Characteristics
Dross Weak petitioner (commoner, low-wisdom mortal) 10–50 gp Cloudy, small, faint glow; used in bulk for low-grade magical fuel
Common Average petitioner 50–200 gp Clear, steady glow; standard trade unit
Choice Strong-willed petitioner (classed NPC, devout worshipper) 200–1,000 gp Bright, warm glow; prized for enchantment and binding rituals
Prime Exceptional petitioner (heroic mortal, powerful caster, saint) 1,000–5,000 gp Brilliant, almost painful to look at; sings faintly; used in artifact creation
Singular Unique petitioner (legendary hero, demigod remnant, fallen angel) 5,000–50,000 gp Each is unique; named; has properties reflecting the original soul's nature
Paragon Legendary hero, demigod-touched, exceptional caster of cosmic influence 50,000–250,000 gp Quest-tier items; each is a named artifact-equivalent
Mythic True demigod-aspect soul, or mortal who achieved transcendence before harvest 250,000–2,000,000+ gp Multi-campaign-defining; their existence is rumor in the Lower Planes' highest circles

The Soul Trade

The soul trade is the dark engine of the Lower Planes' economy. Night hags are the primary brokers:

  1. Harvesting: Petitioners are captured on the Outer Planes and drained. This process destroys the petitioner permanently — they do not reform.
  2. Processing: Raw soul-essence is condensed into shell form through a ritual known only to night hags and certain powerful fiends.
  3. Grading: Assessed through magical appraisal. Grading soul shells requires Knowledge (planes) 10 ranks; base DC 22.
  4. Sale: Sold in Grey Waste towns (particularly Oinos), Gehenna's forge-cities, Baator's open markets (particularly Dis and Minauros), and through discrete brokers in Sigil's Hive Ward.

Moral Weight

This is not morally neutral. Trading in soul shells is:

  • An evil act in PF1e alignment terms: buying or selling a soul shell shifts a character's alignment one step toward evil (per transaction; max one shift per level)
  • Illegal in Sigil under Harmonium regulations, though Hive Ward enforcement is inconsistent
  • Illegal and violently punished on all Upper Planes
  • Legal and expected on most Lower Planes
  • Legal but stigmatized in the Outlands (gate-town dependent)

GM Guidance: Never let soul shell trading become routine or comfortable. If a player begins treating souls as "just another commodity," the game world should push back.

§ 2.6.2 BELIEF TOKENS ▾

Faith Made Tangible

What They Are

On the Outer Planes, belief has literal, physical power. Belief Tokens are crystallized fragments of that conviction — small, faceted crystals that glow with a color corresponding to their alignment and philosophical content. They don't contain a specific belief ("Pelor is the one true sun god") — they contain conviction itself, the raw force of a mortal caring deeply about something.

Formation

Belief Tokens form naturally at locations of intense faith: temples, shrines, sites of martyrdom. They can also be manufactured by:

  • Clerics and oracles (CL 7+) through a 4-hour meditation ritual (produces 1d4 tokens of the caster's alignment, each worth 10–50 gp)
  • Proxy servants of powers as part of their divine duties
  • Philosophical institutions (particularly the Believers of the Source and the Sign of One) through collective meditation

§ 2.6.4 CHAOS SHARDS ▾

Crystallized Possibility

Chaos Shards are fragments of Limbo's ever-shifting matter, temporarily stabilized. They appear as irregular crystals of shifting, iridescent color.

The Volatility Mechanic

Base Value: When first acquired, establish base value: 1d6 × 10 gp (range 10–60 gp per shard).

Transaction Volatility: Every time a Chaos Shard changes hands, roll 1d6:

Roll Multiplier Result
1 ×0 The shard destabilizes and becomes worthless mud/vapor. Gone.
2 ×0.5 Half base value. The shard is flickering, unstable.
3–4 ×1.0 Base value. Normal.
5 ×1.5 The shard is vibrant, pulsing with potential. Above-average value.
6 ×2.0 The shard has momentarily crystallized into something remarkable. Double value.

Stabilization: Order's Wrath, a githzerai monk's Ki stabilization (DC 20 concentration check, 1 minute), or containment in a lead-lined Mechanus-forged box can lock a Chaos Shard's value at its current roll for 24 hours.

Who Accepts Them:

  • Limbo: Standard currency; everyone accepts the volatility
  • Sigil: Specialist dealers and Xaositects only; most merchants refuse
  • Mechanus: Absolutely not. A modron would sooner swallow a gear.
  • Other Planes: Accepted only by planar traders willing to gamble

§ 2.6.7 DIVINE MARKS AND CELESTIAL COMMISSIONS ▾

The Economy of the Gods

For PCs operating at level 15+, a new category of metaphysical currency becomes relevant: direct economic interaction with divine beings.

Divine Marks: A formal acknowledgment from a deity that a specific being has their favor. Not transferable; cannot be spent directly. But a PC bearing a Divine Mark of Pelor can expect:

  • Discounts at all Pelor-aligned institutions (10–30%)
  • Access to services normally requiring clerical status
  • Immunity from lower-tier divine servants who recognize the mark
  • Complications with opposed deities who see it

Divine Commission (value 50,000–500,000 gp): A direct request from a deity for a specific service. Completion earns the equivalent value in divine blessings, which translate to mechanical benefits (GM's discretion) and social standing with the deity's portfolio.

Intercession Request (value 10,000+ gp): A formal request that a deity speak on your behalf to another power. Requires divine relationship; equivalent to an extremely high-level favor.

§ 2.7.3 Three-Question Conversion Protocol ▾

When a PC wants to buy something in an unfamiliar plane, answer three questions:

  1. What currency do they have? (If jinx in Sigil → no friction, done.)
  2. What currency does the seller want? (Match → done. Mismatch → apply friction from II.7.1.)
  3. What plane? (Apply PPM from Module III. That's the price adjustment for the item itself.)

If all three have answers, you have a complete transaction. If any is ambiguous, default to: "apply 10% friction and continue."

TIER 0 — DESTITUTE (0–9 gp) ▾

What this looks like: Nothing. Perhaps the clothes on your back, a bowl for begging, and whatever you can scavenge. On Sigil, this means the Hive — sleeping in alleys, eating whatever the Sensates' charitable kitchens provide. On Athas, it means dying slowly of thirst. On the Grey Waste, it means you're already a petitioner and don't quite remember having had more.

Economic Activities:

  • Begging (1d6 cp per day in a generous city; nothing in most places)
  • Day labor for food (no pay; just survival)
  • Scavenging
  • Desperate barter of personal possessions

Storage: Your pockets. You have nothing to store.

Sigil specifics: The Revolutionary League maintains soup-kitchens and safe-houses that serve Tier 0 individuals without charge. The Bleak Cabal does likewise, out of a grim sense of obligation.   • Brief planar travel (a portal trip to the Outlands and back, with expenses)

Storage: Temple banking or merchant-house letters of credit become practical. A Consortium Vault Account is not yet available (minimum 10,000 jinx).

TIER 5 — WEALTHY (16,000–35,999 gp) ▾

TIER 9 — ULTRA-WEALTHY (760,001–5,000,000 gp) ▾

Beyond WBL — Narrative Construct

At this point, wealth has transcended individual purchasing power and become institutional. The ultra-wealthy don't spend their money — they operate it. Their wealth is infrastructure: trade routes, properties, organizations, political relationships.

Who Is Here:

  • City rulers of significant cities
  • Senior faction leaders (Factol-level)
  • Minor planar lords (the lord of a single Outlands gate-town, for example)
  • The wealthiest mortal merchants in Sigil
  • Lesser yugoloth generals
  • Senior archdevils of lower-ranking layers

What Ultra-Wealth Enables:

Personal Services:

  • Full-time bodyguard detail of high-level fighters or casters (6–12 individuals, 20,000–50,000 gp/year)
  • Personal advisor staff (spymaster, legal counsel, diplomatic representative, trade director: 30,000–80,000 gp/year total)
  • A private portal to Sigil from wherever you call home

Intelligence Networks:

  • A single-plane intelligence network: 15–30 agents, cover identities, safe-houses, communication infrastructure. Cost: 100,000–250,000 gp to establish; 30,000–75,000 gp/year to maintain.
  • Information from: significant political events, notable commerce movements, personality profiles of relevant figures, advance warning of threats
  • A multi-plane intelligence network (3–5 planes): 500,000–2,000,000 gp to establish; 150,000–400,000 gp/year to maintain

Military Capability:

  • A company of high-quality mercenaries (100 fighters, level 5–8): 50,000 gp/month active service; 10,000 gp/month retainer
  • A small private army (500 troops, mixed): 200,000 gp/month active; 40,000 gp/month retainer
  • Commissioning specialist units: Yugoloth platoon (CR 10+): 15,000 gp/operation; Celestial honor guard (if alignment permits): 10,000 gp/month; Elemental company (if faction stands permit): 20,000 gp/month

Property and Infrastructure:

  • A planar waystation (a secure building in a specific plane, with portal access): 200,000–500,000 gp
  • A private demiplane (small; stable; requires a CL 20 caster + materials): 1,000,000+ gp
  • A fully staffed merchant complex in Sigil (10 stalls, warehouse, private office): 300,000–500,000 gp

Banking and Investment:

  • Full Consortium Private Banking with investment portfolio
  • Consortium portfolio returns: 3–8% annually on managed funds
  • Demiplane bond investment (backing stable demiplane creation projects): 8–12% annually (higher risk)

Storage: Foundry Monolith. Consortium portfolio. Real property. The ultra-wealthy hold most of their wealth in illiquid assets (properties, relationships, routes) rather than coins.

TIER 12 — COSMIC WEALTH (500,000,001+ gp) ▾

Reality-Scale Wealth

Who Is Here:

  • Greater powers (CR 30+, the actual gods of the multiverse)
  • The Lady of Pain (implicit; her wealth is the wealth of Sigil itself)
  • Asmodeus's ninth-layer treasury
  • The most ancient beings in the multiverse

At this tier, the concept of "wealth" as discrete units of currency becomes a category error. These entities don't have bank accounts — they have planes. They don't spend money — they move resources. Their "wealth" is the capacity to reshape reality, redirect cosmic forces, or simply do what lesser beings must pay for.

For GM Reference Only:

A greater power's annual "revenue" in divine worship, territorial taxation, and cosmic tribute might be in the range of 10,000,000,000–100,000,000,000 gp equivalent. These numbers are not for PCs to access, arbitrage, or meaningfully interact with except as forces of nature.

The only PC interaction with Tier 12 wealth is as supplicants, adventurers seeking boons, or (at the very highest levels) as individuals who have done something so significant that a Tier 12 entity regards them as worth noticing.

§ 2.9.6 PLANE-SPECIFIC MONEY-CHANGING ▾

City of Brass (Plane of Fire): The Marketplace of Eternal Flame is staffed by efreet who handle currency exchange for visitors. Rates: 10%. High fee reflects the efreeti view that doing business with outsiders is a favor to the outsider.

Great Dismal Delve (Plane of Earth): The Silent Exchange provides currency services for non-dao visitors. Rates 8–12%; services are reliable.

Azure-Below-Azure (Plane of Water): The House of Tides is the most sophisticated banking institution on the Plane of Water, offering full services at 5% exchange rates plus 1% annual storage.

Aaqa (Plane of Air): The Skyport Consortium provides pleasant and efficient services at 3% exchange rates.

Upper Planes (Celestia, Bytopia, Arcadia): Each has institutional banking — the Archon Archives on Celestia, the Gnomish Commercial Cooperatives on Bytopia, the Civic Offices on Arcadia. All are reliable, well-regulated, and welcoming of good-aligned customers.

Lower Planes: The Iron Banks of Dis on Baator provide sophisticated banking for those willing to sign the contracts. The Gehenna Mercenary Exchange handles yugoloth-adjacent commerce. On the Grey Waste, night hag operations dominate. On Carceri, the Abyss, and Pandemonium, there is no reliable money-changing — bring what you need.

Shadowfell: Gloomwrought hosts the Dim Vault — a large fetchling-run operation. Rates: 5% exchange; limited banking for Shadow-Silk and Nightshilling storage. Reliable within the Shadowfell; letters of credit not valid outside.

Feywild: Fey markets exist, but Fey Bargains are the real currency. Formal money-changing is offered only at the neutral markets that drift between Summer and Winter Courts. Rates: 10% plus whatever additional "consideration" the fey merchant decides is appropriate. The "friction" of fey commerce is often not the monetary cost but the intangible cost (lost memory, borrowed time, etc.).

Domains of Dread: Most domains have local money-changers but few handle cross-plane commerce. Outside wealth is often simply refused or heavily discounted. The Domains' economic sealing is part of their metaphysical nature.

Athas: No formal banking; no exchange infrastructure beyond peer-to-peer barter.

§ 2.9.8 EMERGENCY CURRENCY CONVERSION ▾

When a PC is stranded without accessible money-changing infrastructure:

  1. Barter directly: Trade goods or services for what you need. See Module V for labor rates.
  2. Sell minor magical items to local spellcasters: A potion or scroll is universally valued.
  3. Gem-based payment: Gems are near-universally accepted, though you'll lose 15–30% on conversion.
  4. Pay in favors: "I'll repay you when I can." Requires Charisma checks and creates future obligations.
  5. Use a Tier 6+ NPC's influence: Call in a favor with a wealthy patron or factional ally.
  6. Contact the Revolutionary League: They maintain emergency aid networks for certain categories of traveler, though their help always comes with expected future reciprocity.

GM Advice: Currency emergencies are valuable story beats. A PC suddenly unable to pay for something they need creates drama that predictable wealth management cannot. Use it — but don't abuse it.

  • 1 Jinx = 1 gp (universal)
  • 1 Styx-Iron Bar = 50 gp (Lower Planes + Sigil)

  • Same zone → 0–5% friction
  • Adjacent zones → 5–10% friction
  • Opposed zones → 10–20% friction
  • "Wrong currency for this plane" → 15–25% friction
  • Domains of Dread out → 20–30% (if at all)

  • Air: Zephyr Mark = 1 gp (5% friction off-plane)
  • Earth: Earth-Coin = 0.9 gp (10% friction off-plane)

  • Good Planes: Celestian Trade Bars preferred
  • Evil Planes: Styx-Iron preferred
  • Mechanus: Gear-Marks (perfect 1:1 with jinx)
  • Limbo: Chaos Shards (roll for value)
  • Outlands: Everything works

  • Tier 1–3: Normal adventuring party, levels 1–5
  • Tier 4–6: Mid-campaign, levels 5–12
  • Tier 7–8: Endgame, levels 12–20 (WBL cap = Tier 8)

  1. What currency do they have? (If jinx in Sigil → no friction, done.)

  3. What plane? (Apply PPM from Module III.)

  • In Sigil: everything works, small friction, done.
  • Outside Sigil: apply friction, check PPM, make it a story beat.
  • On a hostile plane with wrong currency: make it an adventure.
  • Tier 9+ PC: the campaign is different now. Adventures are political and institutional.
